## 1. Campaign Concept — The Big Idea

**Concept name: "You're Already Watching This."**

The truest sentence in Blog 3 and the whole strategic move. The facilitator is already observing. They already see the learner lean in, hear the clarifying question that means someone is testing the framework against their own job, feel the room go with them or against them. And they have watched that knowledge evaporate at the end of every session because nobody gave them a way to keep it. The concept names that loss, then resolves it: you do not need to notice *more*. You need a structure for what you already notice, and three to five minutes after the session to write it down.

"You're already watching this" does three jobs: **recognition** (I see you, you already do this), **relief** (the fix is not one more thing while you teach), and **a method** (five behaviors, and when to capture them). That triple move is the antidote to the reflex every facilitator has the instant you mention observation — "I can't take notes while I teach." Correct. You do not. That objection is planted early and answered in full, never a footnote.

**Strategic territory:** the facilitator as the primary, systematic evaluation-data source — genuinely underclaimed. **Emotional territory:** quiet professional frustration ("my best evidence keeps disappearing"), resolving into recognition ("someone finally named the thing I do"), settling into practical confidence ("I could run this Tuesday"). One experienced facilitator talking to another about what actually works in the room.

**What the creative must make the audience feel:** *You are not missing a skill. You already have the instinct. What you have been missing is a way to keep what you see, so it becomes evidence instead of a memory that fades by the parking lot. Here are the five things to watch for, here is when to write them down, and no, you do not do it while you teach.*

**What this week is NOT doing:**
- NOT re-opening the measurement gap or re-arguing Weeks 1–2. The Dye research is one line of context at most, never a lead.
- NOT framing the protocol as a task during delivery. The single costliest failure available. Any card, line, or image of a facilitator filling a form or splitting attention mid-session argues against us.
- NOT leadership register. It talks over the practitioner to a funder who is not the primary audience this week.
- NOT turning the illustrative example into data. Blog 3's "8 of 12 managers" is a teaching hypothetical. If used, labeled an example. Never an InSync result.

## 7. Audience Insight — The North Star

**The insight:** the dominant emotion is **quiet professional frustration that their best evidence keeps disappearing,** paired with earned defensiveness about anything that splits attention while they teach. They already know their sessions produce signal and have watched it evaporate for years. What they lack is a structure for what they already see, and permission to capture it *after* rather than *during*.

**The creative opportunity:** name the invisible skill out loud and hand them a way to keep it. "You're already watching this. You just haven't been writing it down." Relief, then a method concrete enough to run Tuesday. A save/screenshot means they intend to use it. Validate first, always — *you already do this*, never *you should be doing more*.

**Prioritization:** serve **Practitioners** when audiences conflict. The practitioner is the actor; the leader is the sponsor. Serve leadership through exactly one channel (the newsletter's single program-intelligence line + the webinar). Do not add a boardroom layer elsewhere.

**The north-star question:** *"Would a working facilitator read this and think 'that's exactly what I already do, and now I have a way to keep it' — or 'this is someone telling me to add another task while I teach'?"* If the latter, rework it.
