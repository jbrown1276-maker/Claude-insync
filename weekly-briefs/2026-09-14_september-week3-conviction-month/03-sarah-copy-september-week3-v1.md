## 2. LinkedIn Post 2 — "the room is the dataset" reframe

*Single sharp standalone post. Built for the save. Saks & Belcort sourced inline.*

**Copy**

The post-course survey asks a learner if they will use the framework.

In the training glow, almost everyone says yes. Their intentions are genuinely good.

Research from Saks & Belcort shows only 30 to 40% actually apply what they learn without reinforcement.

So the survey measures intention. The room measures thinking.

Watch whether a learner works through how they would use the idea. Watch the question that reveals they are already applying it to their own job. That behavior predicts transfer far better than "I'll use this" ever will.

The best data was never in the survey.
It was in the room the whole time.

## 4. Weekly Newsletter

**Subject line options (sentence case)**
- **A (recognition):** your facilitators are your best evaluation data, and most never tap it
- **B (reframe):** the best data in your program is already in the room
- **C (relief):** you are already watching for this, you just haven't been writing it down

**Preview text (paired)**
- **A:** they see what lands and what doesn't. Then it disappears at the end of the session. Here is how to keep it.
- **B:** five behaviors that tell you learning is happening, and when to write them down.
- **C:** no, you don't take notes while you teach. Here is what you do instead.

**Body**

Your facilitators are your best source of evaluation data. Most organizations never tap it.

They watch what lands and what does not. They hear the question that means someone is testing the idea against their own job. They feel the room go with them or against them. And then that knowledge disappears at the end of the session, because nobody gave them a way to keep it.

That is the loss. It is quiet, it happens every week, and it is fixable.

You do not need your facilitators to notice more. They already notice. What they need is a structure for what they already see, and three to five minutes to write it down.

There are five behaviors that tell you learning is happening. A learner applies the framework to their own situation, unprompted. They ask a clarifying question about a specific scenario. They name the gap between their current approach and the framework. They push back and think it through instead of nodding. They teach each other. A trained eye catches these in real time.

And no, you do not take notes while you teach. You separate the moments. During a transition, at lunch, at the end of the day, you spend a few minutes noting the patterns you already saw. If a producer supports the session, they can capture in real time. Teaching and documenting are two different moments, and this only works when you keep them apart.

Here is what that gives you over time. Session one tells you what worked. By session five, you know which scenarios landed and which examples stuck, without waiting six months for a survey to come back. What one facilitator sees becomes what the whole program knows.
