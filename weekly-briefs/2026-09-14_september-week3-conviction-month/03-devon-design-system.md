## 1. Visual Concept Summary

"You're Already Watching This." Week 1 put a number on the record. Week 2 stated exactly what it shows. Both spoke from the boardroom, in the register of the exhibit. Week 3 walks down to the floor of the live session and speaks facilitator to facilitator. The surface does not change — deep-navy ground, teal rationed to what matters, white space as a material — but the subject changes: this week the hero is not a numeral, it is **a behavior**, and teal stops marking a payoff figure and starts marking **the observable moment of learning, the thing the trained eye catches.**

The one new object is a nine-card carousel, and its hardest design problem is not any single card but the through-line: five behavior cards that must read as *one protocol*, not five loose tips. A thin teal thread solves it. It enters on card 2, persists and visibly accumulates across the five behavior cards, and gathers into a single resolved form on the method card — so by the time the reader reaches the method, the five signals have visibly become one system. The thread is an argument, not an ornament. If "this is one system" does not land in under two seconds at thumbnail, the carousel has failed and reads as a generic "5 tips" set.

One discipline governs every card: the protocol is never a task performed while teaching. Any card that depicts capture depicts the *after* moment — never a facilitator splitting attention mid-session. That is the single costliest failure available this week, and the design guards against it as hard as the copy does.

No humans this week. The *room* is the implied subject — abstract, a floor plan, a seating rhythm — never literal classroom stock. The behaviors are the imagery.

## 3. Typography System

Unchanged from Weeks 1–2. Three faces carry forward. One binding rule added: on a behavior card, Face 1 carries the behavior name and the behavior name is the largest element (the role the hero numeral played in Weeks 1–2).

**Face 1 — Data / Display / Headline (grotesque sans).** Inter or Archivo, tabular figures where numbers appear. Behavior name 44–64 px; headline 48–56 px; subhead 24–32 px. Numerals Bold/800; behavior names Semibold/600; subheads Medium/500.

**Face 2 — Interpretive voice (humanist serif).** Source Serif or Newsreader. Carries the "what it looks like in the room" line on each behavior card (24–34 px) and the Bozarth pull-quote on Post 3. The serif is the voice; the sans is the evidence. Never carries a number.

**Face 3 — Source Stamp (mono / small-caps).** IBM Plex Mono or Space Mono. Carries the source stamp ONLY, on exactly three surfaces this week (the Saks & Belcort stamp, the Brandon Hall stamp; the "illustration" label is NOT mono — see below).

**Header label ("what to watch for" / protocol name):** Face 1 Medium small (16–20 px), quiet, top-left, muted white (~88%), sentence case. A running protocol title, not a citation — never mono, never teal.

**The "illustration, not a client result" label, if used:** Face 1 Medium at reading weight (14–16 px), NOT mono. Mono would read as a source stamp and imply data — the exact accident to avoid.

Sentence case throughout. Tabular figures only where a stat appears.

## 4. Core Visual Motif — The Carousel Card System + the Teal-Thread Device

Two devices working together: the fixed card template (five behaviors read as one set) and the teal thread (they read as one *protocol*). A sibling to the source stamp (Week 1, "who says so") and the conditions bracket (Week 2, "what does it prove"); Week 3 answers "what do I watch for, and how is this one system."

### 4a. The fixed card template
One template, 1080 × 1080. Vary the content, never the chrome. Safe zone 72 px all sides. Navy `#032B43` full bleed. Five fixed zones, positions locked across all behavior cards:
1. **Header label** — top-left, quiet. Protocol name / "what to watch for." Face 1 Medium 16–20 px, muted white. On every card (a screenshotted card still announces it belongs to a protocol).
2. **Behavior name** — the hero. Face 1 Semibold 44–64 px, white, left-aligned, upper-middle third. Largest element on the card.
3. **"What it looks like in the room" line** — Face 2 serif 24–34 px, off-white, directly below the name, generous leading. One sentence.
4. **Progress indicator** — bottom edge, nine small marks; current card teal and slightly wider, rest muted gray. The count + "there's more" signal. NOT the teal thread.
5. **Brand mark** — white bug, corner, at or below header-label scale. On every behavior card.
Reserve a narrow vertical channel at the left safe-zone edge for the thread from the first sketch.

### 4b. The teal-thread device
**What it is.** A thin teal vertical spine at the left content edge that persists across cards 3–7 and visually **accumulates**, gathering into one resolved form on card 8. Same family as Week 2's teal spine, new job: threading five behaviors into one system.

**Geometry.** Teal `#00B9F5`, 3 px hero (2 px min thumbnail), left content edge (~48–56 px in). On each behavior card a small teal **signal node** (a short tick or small filled node, ~12–16 px) sits on the thread beside the behavior name. This node IS the "one teal signal per card." Same shape every behavior card.

**Accumulation (the swipe payoff):**
- **Card 2:** the thread *begins* — a short unresolved teal segment, no node yet.
- **Cards 3–7:** the thread runs fuller height; each card adds one node, and the node's vertical position advances one step down per card (card 3 highest, card 7 lowest) — five marks marching down one continuous line. Five nodes, one line: five behaviors, one protocol.
- **Card 8:** the thread **resolves** — five nodes gathered on one spine, terminating in a single stronger teal form (five signals → one protocol).
- **Card 9:** the thread hands off to the teal CTA — the line has become the invitation.

**Never:** a decorative left border (it must change card to card or it is ornament); a progress bar (that is the bottom indicator — keep distinct); a numbered "5 steps" spine or icons on nodes (nodes are plain teal marks); louder than the behavior name.

**Scaling / thumbnail.** At 150 px the thread must read as one continuous teal line with marks — 2 px min, nodes ≥8 px. Squint-test every card at 150 px: you should see (a) this is a behavior card in a protocol, (b) it is part of a teal-threaded set, before reading a word.

**Card-count note.** Nine-card structure is a Jeff confirm item (§9). The enter → accumulate (5 nodes) → resolve → hand-off arc is built for nine; if the count changes, node spacing rescales but the arc holds.

## 5. Photography and Imagery Direction

**Same discipline. No human subjects, no stock.** The shift: the *room* is the implied subject — abstract, never literal. Where a card wants a spatial cue, use an abstracted floor plan or seating rhythm in navy-on-navy or muted gray, standing where the facilitator stands. Never a photographed/illustrated classroom, never people, never a webinar-platform screenshot.

**The anti-image, banned outright:** any card showing a facilitator taking notes, filling a form, holding a clipboard, or splitting attention mid-session. Where a card depicts capture (card 8), it depicts the *after* moment — a quiet artifact of a few noted patterns, calm, post-session.

**Grading.** Navy-dominant, teal-signal, muted-gray for the lost register. Flat, calm, high-legibility. No drama lighting, no glow, no red/alarm. Off-white paper allowed only for a quiet exhibit framing (card 8 artifact).

**Charts / stats.** Only Brandon Hall 77% (card 7, optional) and Saks & Belcort 30–40% (Post 2, optional), each with its source stamp. No invented trend lines, no fake dashboards. Never render the 8-of-12 hypothetical as a chart or data.

## 7. Layout Principles

1. The behavior gets the room (behavior name is the largest element on a behavior card).
2. One template, varied content (identical chrome; only the behavior changes — the "one protocol" argument).
3. The teal thread is an argument, not a border (must change/accumulate card to card).
4. One teal signal per card (the node, or the CTA).
5. Gray is what is lost, white + teal is what is caught (squint-test before the words).
6. Every card stands alone (header label + brand mark + protocol logic on every behavior card).
7. Earn the swipe (card 1 hooks at thumbnail; every card ends with a reason to advance).
8. Capture is always the after moment (never a facilitator writing mid-session).
9. White space is a feature (72 px card / 80 px header margins; one idea per card).
10. Thumbnail-first (validate the full carousel at 150 px; "one system" lands in under two seconds).

## 8. What to Avoid

- Any card showing a facilitator taking notes / filling a form / splitting attention mid-session — the anti-message. Capture is the after moment only (card 8).
- A generic "5 tips" carousel look — the teal thread + fixed template must make "one system" land in under two seconds.
- Rendering the 8-of-12 hypothetical as data — label at reading weight or leave off. Never a chart, never a mono stamp.
- Reviving 94-vs-13 / the two 73%s / "60% decline" / "17 industries" / "14% long-term" as a hero.
- Inventing a client number to fill a card — none exist; white space and a sharper behavior line instead.
- The teal thread as a progress bar or decorative rule — horizontal bottom marks = progress; vertical teal thread = protocol logic; the thread must accumulate.
- Icons on nodes / checkmark-x / green-red / traffic-light logic — nodes are plain teal marks.
- Teal used more than once per card, or for the lost/survey register — gray is what is lost; teal is only what is caught.
- Golden Honey, a fourth accent, red/alarm, stock, fake dashboards, literal classroom imagery, human subjects.
- Brand mark louder than the behavior.
- Register drift in newsletter/Email #3 visuals — no boardroom exhibit grandeur.
- A named webinar host, an invented webinar title/takeaways, or the Bozarth byline attributed to Hofmann — card 9 is date + URL only; Post 3 is Dr. Jane Bozarth only.
- Claiming the Companion Guide is free/ungated or gated in any button label or header copy until Jeff confirms.
