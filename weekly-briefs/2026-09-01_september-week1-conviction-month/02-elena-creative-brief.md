## 1. Campaign Concept — The Big Idea

**Concept name: "On the Record."**

The research is on the record. Two hundred L&D practitioners went on the record about how they actually evaluate, under an independent researcher with academic and evaluation credentials. Every number we publish this week appears on the record, with its source attached as a structural part of the design, never a footnote. And the payoff for the reader is that they walk away with something *they* can put on the record: a defensible, sourced sentence they can carry into a budget conversation and repeat to a CFO without flinching.

"On the record" is doing three jobs at once. It means **documented** (this is data, not a claim). It means **attributable** (here is exactly who said it and how many). And it means **portable** (a leader can lift it into their own executive narrative). That triple meaning is the antidote to the self-report trap: on-the-record evidence is the opposite of marketing.

**Strategic territory:** conviction through independent research. **Emotional territory:** the sequence Marcus named — credible relief ("someone finally studied this, I'm not imagining it"), then uncomfortable clarity ("I cannot un-see this gap"), settling into resolve ("and the shift is learnable"). The register is a person who has the data and does not need to raise their voice.

**What the creative must make the audience feel and recognize:** *You are not failing personally. The gap is industry-wide, it is documented, and the difference between programs that work and programs that only look like they work is not budget or talent. It is how they evaluate. Here is the proof, and here is the turn.*

**What this week is NOT doing:**
- NOT re-teaching the measurement gap. The reader met it in July. Meeting it again reads as "haven't we covered this?" and kills momentum.
- NOT leading with the InSync solution. The expertise-modified CIPP approach, Managed Services, and client case stats (400+ sessions, 30% lifts) are secondary social proof at most. They never lead. Leading with them converts a research story into a sales pitch.
- NOT the full 5 Shifts teaching. The Shifts appear as the bridge and the forward promise, not this week's main lesson. The teaching lands in the Companion Guide (Sep 8) and the webinar (Sep 24).
- NOT alarmist. This is conviction, not fear. The reader should feel equipped, never scolded.

## 2. Creative Pillars

Four pillars. Every asset should express at least one; the hero assets express several.

**Pillar 1 — Sourced, not said.**
*Definition:* Evidence registers before argument. We show the reader the research — the survey, the sample, the independent researcher — so they process "this was studied" before "here is a point of view." Attribution is not a caveat; it is the credibility.
*Per channel:* Devon renders the source line as a fixed structural element on every stat, not an afterthought. Sarah attaches the attribution to every number, every time. Newsletter and email open on the finding, not on InSync. LinkedIn Teaser positions InSync as the org that *ran* the research.

**Pillar 2 — The uncomfortable contrast.**
*Definition:* One stark, honest juxtaposition the reader cannot un-see: what organizations measure (94% satisfaction) against what matters (13% relevant to business outcomes). Not alarmist. Just impossible to file away.
*Per channel:* This is the week's most amplifiable idea and gets its own designed object (Springboard B). Devon builds the "94 vs 13" card as a self-contained, screenshot-ready artifact. It anchors Wednesday's LinkedIn post, appears in the newsletter, and is Devon's single most important deliverable of the week.

**Pillar 3 — The wrong question, not bad data.**
*Definition:* The reframe that supplies the intellectual turn. The problem is not "collect more data." It is that evaluation starts from easy-to-collect data instead of from the business objective. The exceptions start from the outcome and design measurement backward. This is where diagnosis becomes conviction.
*Per channel:* Sarah's Blog-adjacent copy and the Thursday LinkedIn post carry the turn. Devon can express it visually as a directional reframe (the easy data everyone collects vs. the question the exceptions ask first). It should feel like a click of recognition hardening into resolve, never a scold.

**Pillar 4 — Built to be carried.**
*Definition:* The reader should be able to lift our evidence into their own argument. A save on LinkedIn (a leader keeping the stat for their own budget conversation) is worth more than a like. The creative is designed to travel without us.
*Per channel:* Devon: self-contained assets, source baked in, legible at thumbnail size, screenshot-clean. Sarah: one sentence per asset that is portable — quotable, sourced, and repeatable by someone who is not us. The 5 Shifts appear here as the forward promise: the path the reader carries toward the Companion Guide and webinar.

## 3. Visual Direction (for Devon)

This is a new chapter. Build a **conviction identity** around evidence, data, and the idea of the record. Do not reference the Q3 incompleteness line, and do not use Golden Honey in any capacity this month.

**Color application.** Navy and teal is the palette, per the CB. Assign each color a job so the palette argues the strategy:
- **Deep navy** — the primary ground. Authority, proof, seriousness, the "on the record" surface. Use it for hero backgrounds and for the evidence exhibits. This is the color of a case that has been made.
- **Teal (InSync Cool Blue register)** — the signal color. Reserve it for *what matters*: the 13%, the business outcome, the exception, the turn, the CTA. Teal is the payoff. Do not spend it on decoration; when teal appears, it means "this is the thing."
- **Muted gray / desaturated navy** — the "easy data everyone collects." Render the 94% satisfaction figure, completion rates, and the filed-away metrics in this muted register. The contrast between muted-gray 94% and teal 13% *is* the argument, carried in color before a word is read.
- **White / off-white** — treated as a material, not a background. White space is a feature this month (CB). Give the numbers room to breathe. Crowding kills the "settled confidence" register.
- **Rule:** no third accent color. No Golden Honey. The discipline of navy + teal + gray + white is the point; a fourth hue dilutes the signal logic.

**Typography.** Confident and editorial. Large numerals are the heroes — set stats big and let them carry the composition. Pair a clean grotesque/sans for data and short-form with a more human voice for interpretive lines if a second face is warranted. Running copy is **sentence case** throughout (this matches Sarah's copy rule; keep the systems aligned). The **source line** gets its own persistent typographic treatment — small, quiet, always present, ideally in a mono or small-caps register so it reads as a citation stamp, not body text.

**Imagery.** Abstract data and evidence only. Favor honest charts, exhibit-like framings, and the artifact-of-record feel. **Never shoot or source:** generic stock (no diverse-team-high-fiving, no lightbulbs, no gears, no handshake-over-a-laptop), and never mock up a fake product dashboard that implies an InSync software screenshot. The research is the imagery. If a chart appears, it must be a real, defensible representation of the verified data — no invented trend lines, no chart junk.

**Iconography.** Simple line marks for four ideas: measurement, decision, evidence, performance (CB). Keep them structural and quiet. **Core motif — the "source stamp":** a fixed teal rule or exhibit-mark that sits beneath every published figure and carries its citation. It is the visual embodiment of "on the record" and the through-line that makes the week read as one system. Let its *state* track the three-beat arc: Recognition (the stamp under a question or a muted figure), Evidence (the stamp under the stark contrast), Conviction (the stamp under the teal payoff and the path forward).

**Layout principles.** White space as a feature. One idea per asset. The number gets the room; the source gets its fixed place; the CTA gets teal. Legible at thumbnail — assume every asset will be seen small first.

**The hero object.** Build the **"94 vs 13" card** (Springboard B) as the week's flagship designed object: self-contained, screenshot-ready, source line baked in, muted-gray 94 against teal 13, honest and stark. This is the most amplifiable single asset of the week. Give it your best hour.

**What to avoid, and why.**
- Golden Honey or the incompleteness line → they belong to a chapter that closed; carrying them forward muddies the fresh conviction identity.
- A fourth accent color → breaks the color-as-argument logic (muted = easy data, teal = what matters).
- Fake dashboards / product screenshots → reads as a vendor demo and trips the self-report trap.
- Alarmist visual treatment (red, danger, shock) → this is conviction, not fear.
- Any stat without its source stamp → a naked number reads as marketing; a sourced number reads as research. This is non-negotiable.
- **Do not render "17 industries" or "14% long-term outcome data" anywhere.** Both are unverified (Section 7). If you need a base figure, use "N=200 practitioners plus 24 interviews."

## 4. Tone of Voice (for Sarah)

**Governing principle, one sentence:** State what the research found, let the evidence carry the weight, and never raise your voice.

**What it sounds like.**

*Newsletter (widest reach, leadership-first):*
> Most teams are measuring the wrong thing. The research says so. We surveyed 200 L&D practitioners about how they evaluate training. 94% collect learner satisfaction. Only 13% collect data relevant to business outcomes. (InSync 2026 industry survey of L&D evaluation practices, Dr. Charles Dye, N=200 plus 24 interviews.) The gap is not a talent problem. It is a question problem. Here is what the exceptions do differently.

*Email #1 (single ask, highest intent):*
> You are collecting evaluation data. Is any of it answering the business question? In our 2026 survey, 83% of practitioners said their data gives an incomplete or inaccurate picture of the training. They are not wrong. The full research shows why, and what the programs that work do instead.

*LinkedIn, Wednesday (the stat post):*
> 94% of L&D teams collect satisfaction data. 13% collect data relevant to business outcomes. That is not a data problem. It is a question problem. (InSync 2026 industry survey, Dr. Charles Dye, N=200 plus 24 interviews.)

*LinkedIn, Thursday (the turn):*
> The exceptions are not better funded. They start from a different question. They name the business outcome first, then design the measurement backward from it. That is the shift. The research report shows where most organizations break, and the five shifts that close the gap.

**What to avoid — wrong vs. right.**

- **Re-teaching.** Wrong: "As we've discussed, the measurement gap means..." Right: open on the finding, not a recap. The reader already knows the gap exists.
- **Self-promotion as evidence.** Wrong: "Our proven approach transforms how you measure." Right: "The research documents the gap. The exceptions start from the business outcome."
- **Naked stats.** Wrong: "Only 13% measure what matters." Right: every stat carries its attribution, every time.
- **Em dashes.** Wrong: "The gap is real — and it is not your fault — the research proves it." Right: short declarative sentences. "The gap is real. It is not your fault. The research proves it." **No em dashes in your copy this month.**
- **Banned words.** Do not use: best-in-class, synergy, leverage (as a verb), value-add, holistic, innovative (without specifics), transform, world-class.
- **"Campaign."** Banned in reader-facing copy. Use "this month," "September," or "this conversation."
- **Vendor language.** InSync is a *partner*, never a *vendor*. And never name a webinar host. Use "our team" or "an InSync facilitator." Jennifer Hofmann bylines content; she is not confirmed as the host.
- **Casing.** Sentence case throughout, including headlines and subject lines where the platform allows.

## 7. Audience Insight — The North Star

**The insight:** This reader's dominant emotion is not confusion. It is exposure. They already suspect their dashboard does not answer the business question, and they walk into budget conversations quietly worried the numbers will not survive scrutiny. What they lack is not awareness of the gap. It is *external validation that the gap is industry-wide and not a personal failing*, plus a credible, defensible story they can repeat to a CFO.

**The creative opportunity:** Independent research names their private worry out loud and hands them ammunition they did not have to generate themselves. "83% feel their data gives an incomplete picture. You are not imagining it." The moment this feels like self-report, the credibility the whole month depends on collapses. The moment it feels like third-party evidence they can carry into their own argument, we have won.

**Prioritization:** When a decision must serve L&D Leadership or Practitioners at the other's expense, **serve Leadership.** The report is the featured asset, leadership is who it converts, and conviction is a leadership emotion this month. Practitioners are amplifiers and the future webinar audience; their payoff (the 5 Shifts, the tools, the Companion Guide) is *previewed* now and *delivered* later. Do not dilute the conviction message to make room for practitioner how-to.

**The north-star question — ask this before finalizing any decision:**
*"Does this make the reader feel handed independent evidence they can carry into their own budget conversation — or does it feel like InSync talking about InSync?"*
If it is the latter, rework it.
