## 1. Campaign Objective and Success Metrics

**Sharpened strategic statement.** September Week 1 exists to convert two months of primed belief into evidenced conviction by putting independent research in front of L&D leaders. July taught the audience to recognize the measurement gap; August gave them the confidence that it is fixable. This week we stop asserting and start proving: we hand them third-party evidence that the difference between programs that work and programs that only look like they work is not budget or talent — it is how they evaluate. The strategic job of Week 1 is to make the Dye research report the credibility spine of the month and the featured download, and to move the reader from "this could work" to "this is what it looks like when it does."

This is a distribution-and-conviction week, not an awareness week. We are not re-opening the problem from scratch. We are producing the evidence that closes it.

## 2. Target Audience Analysis

**Primary activation target — L&D Leadership.** CLO, VP of L&D, Director of Learning, and HR leadership. This is the audience we are activating this week: every primary CTA, the newsletter lead, and the research framing are built for them.

Psychographically, this person is under executive pressure to prove that learning moves the business, and they suspect — correctly — that their current dashboard does not. They collect satisfaction and completion data because it is what the LMS gives them, and they walk into budget conversations quietly worried the numbers will not survive scrutiny. Their dominant emotion is not confusion; it is exposure. They already sense the gap. What they lack is *external validation* that the gap is industry-wide and not a personal failing, and a credible, defensible story they can repeat to a CFO.

What resonates: independent research that names their private suspicion out loud ("83% feel their data gives an incomplete picture — you are not imagining it"); evidence they can lift directly into their own executive narrative; a source of authority that is not InSync marketing itself. What does not resonate: being re-taught the problem they already accepted in July, InSync self-congratulation, or anything that reads as a vendor pitch dressed as a study. The instant this feels like self-report, the credibility that the whole month depends on collapses.

**Secondary amplification target — Practitioners.** Facilitators, instructional designers, learning analysts, training managers. They are not the primary conversion target for the gated report; they are the amplifiers and the future webinar audience. They want tools they can use Monday: the 5 Shifts, the worksheets, the Companion Guide. They resonate with the practical and the peer-validated and are turned off by anything that stays at the strategic altitude without giving them something to *do*.

**Explicit prioritization.** When a message, a CTA, or a design choice must serve one audience at the expense of the other, serve L&D Leadership. The report is the featured asset; leadership is who it converts; conviction is a leadership emotion this month. Practitioners are served as amplification and are handed forward to the webinar and the Companion Guide (live Sep 8) — the practitioner payoff is *previewed* this week and *delivered* later. Do not dilute the leadership conviction message to make room for practitioner how-to; the 5 Shifts appear this week as the bridge and the promise, not as the week's main teaching.

## 3. Messaging Pillars and Hierarchy

**The One Thing (one sentence).** The best-performing learning programs are not lucky — they evaluate differently, and independent research now proves most organizations are measuring the wrong thing.

Everything downstream is a variation on that sentence. The word doing the work this month is *proves*. July said "notice this." August said "you could fix this." September says "here is the evidence."

**Tier 1 — The gap is real, and it is not just you.** *Strategic function:* validation and permission. Lead stat: 94% collect learner satisfaction; only 13% collect data relevant to business outcomes. This is the blunt, shareable contrast that names the reader's private worry as an industry fact. It converts anxiety into recognition and earns the right to everything that follows.

**Tier 2 — The problem is the wrong question, not bad data.** *Strategic function:* reframe. 83% feel their data gives an inaccurate or incomplete picture; 73% cannot articulate how management even uses the data they report. The diagnosis is not "collect more" — it is that evaluation starts from easy-to-collect data instead of from the organizational objective. This is the intellectual core Dye supplies and the reason the research feels true rather than promotional.

**Tier 3 — The exceptions do one thing differently: they start from the business outcome.** *Strategic function:* the turn from problem to path. The programs that work name the business outcome first, then design measurement backward from it. This is where conviction lives — the gap is not fate, it is a practice, and the practice is learnable.

**Tier 4 — The 5 Shifts are how you make the turn.** *Strategic function:* the bridge and the forward promise. Name the 5 Shifts as the practical model that operationalizes Tier 3, and point to the research report (now), the Companion Guide (Sep 8), and the webinar (Sep 24) as where the reader acts. Tier 4 is a signpost this week, not the full teaching.

**The message to AVOID this week, and what it would cost.** Do not re-introduce or re-explain the measurement gap as if the reader is meeting it for the first time, and do not lead with the InSync solution (the expertise-modified CIPP approach, Managed Services, client case stats). Re-explaining the gap wastes the conviction the campaign has spent two months building and makes September sound like July — the audience will read it as "haven't we covered this?" and disengage. Leading with the InSync answer converts an *independent research story* into a *vendor pitch* and forfeits the single asset that makes this month different from every content push a competitor could run: the fact that the proof is not our opinion. Client case stats (400+ sessions, engagement lifts) may appear as secondary social proof only; they must never lead.

## 5. Tone and Positioning Guardrails

**What it must sound like.** Confident, evidence-forward, and calm — the register of someone who has the data and does not need to raise their voice. InSync is the organization that *conducted the research*; the posture is "here is what the industry told us," not "here is what we believe." Concrete examples of the right register: "We surveyed 200 L&D practitioners. 83% told us their data doesn't answer the business question." / "Most teams are measuring the wrong thing. The research says so." / "The exceptions aren't better funded. They start from a different question." Human, clear, and credible — a partner handing a leader something they can use, not a vendor closing.

**What to avoid — specific phrases and registers.** No re-teaching ("As we've discussed, the measurement gap…"). No self-promotion masquerading as evidence ("Our proven approach delivers…"). No hype adjectives without specifics. Enforce the InSync banned list absolutely this month, because a research-credibility play dies on marketing language: do not use **best-in-class, synergy, leverage (as a verb), value-add, holistic, innovative (without specifics), transform, world-class.** InSync is a **partner**, never a **vendor**. Avoid the fear register — this is conviction, not alarm; the reader should feel equipped, not scolded. And do not name a webinar host: use "our team / an InSync facilitator." Jennifer Hofmann bylines content; she is not confirmed as the webinar host, so no copy may say she leads it.

**Attribution guardrail (non-negotiable).** Every research stat appears with its source — "InSync 2026 industry survey of L&D evaluation practices, Dr. Charles Dye (N=200 + 24 interviews)." The attribution is not a footnote; it is the credibility. Naked stats read as marketing; sourced stats read as research.

## 6. Competitive Landscape Summary

**Who else makes this argument.** The "measure impact, not satisfaction" case is not new territory — it is the most crowded conversation in L&D. Kirkpatrick and Phillips have owned the vocabulary for six decades (Dye's own foreword concedes the principle "has been settled since 1959"). ATD, Training Industry, and the LMS/LXP vendors (Docebo, Cornerstone, 360Learning) all publish "prove the ROI of learning" content. Measurement platforms (Watershed, Explorance/Metrics That Matter) sell dashboards against exactly this pain. The argument that satisfaction scores are insufficient is table stakes; nearly everyone in the category asserts it.

**InSync's differentiated angle.** InSync is not asserting the argument — it *ran the primary research that quantifies it*. That is the entire edge this month. Almost every competitor cites the same secondary literature or their own product telemetry; InSync commissioned an independent, mixed-methods survey (N=200 + 24 interviews) under a named external researcher with academic and evaluation credentials (Dr. Charles Dye, Ph.D., J.D.). The proof is not self-report and not borrowed — it is InSync's own third-party evidence. Second differentiator: InSync ties the diagnosis specifically to *live learning* (virtual/hybrid), where real-time behavioral observation makes the 5 Shifts uniquely executable — a claim async-first and platform competitors cannot make. The positioning is: everyone else has an opinion about the gap; InSync has the data on it.

**Competitive risks.** (1) *Sameness at a glance.* Because the topic is so crowded, a reader skimming a stat card may file it as "more ROI content." Mitigation: lead with the independence and specificity of the research, not the generic claim. (2) *Credibility scrutiny.* Any leader who reads closely will note the survey used convenience sampling (MTurk + InSync's community) — a genuine methodological limit the report itself discloses. We do not hide it, but we do not foreground methodology in short-form; we lead with the findings and let the full report carry the caveats. (3) *The self-report trap.* The single biggest competitive own-goal would be letting the research read as an InSync sales instrument. Frame every touch as industry diagnosis; the moment it reads as promotion, InSync forfeits the one thing competitors cannot copy.
