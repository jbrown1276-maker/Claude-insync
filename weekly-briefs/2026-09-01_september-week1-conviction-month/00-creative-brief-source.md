1. Project Overview

Week 1 opens September, Conviction Month, by grounding the Proof Over Promises narrative in independent research. The anchor asset is InSync’s industry survey of L&D evaluation practices (Dr. Charles “Chip” Dye), which documents a hard, industry-wide truth: most organizations collect evaluation data and never connect it to business outcomes. That gap is the proof. The InSync answer is the shift from measuring satisfaction to measuring impact.
Blog 1 introduces what high-performing programs actually have in common: they evaluate differently. The tone shifts from confidence to conviction. We are not claiming outcomes, we are showing the data on why most evaluation fails and what the exceptions do instead.
This creative brief guides Week 1 creation of: Blog 1; Learning Leadership Series Article (open — see flags); Weekly Newsletter; LinkedIn Newsletter Teaser; LinkedIn Posts (3, T/W/TH); Email #1.

Purpose
Open September by proving, with third-party research, that the difference between programs that work and programs that only look like they work is how they evaluate. Move the audience from “this could work” to “this is what it looks like when it does.”

Move the audience from confidence (August) to conviction (September). Blog 1 and the research establish that the outcome difference is not luck or talent, it is evaluation practice. The Dye research report is the credibility spine. The webinar and Companion Guide give practitioners the 5 Shifts they can act on.

4. Pain Point Framework

Operational
•  Collecting evaluation data that never answers the business question the training was meant to serve.
•  No framework for turning satisfaction and completion data into design decisions.
•  Difficulty distinguishing programs that worked from programs that just looked like they worked.
Emotional
•  Suspecting the current evaluation approach is not telling the whole story, and being right.
•  Skepticism from leadership about whether the numbers reported mean anything.
•  Anxiety of presenting outcomes data without confidence it will hold up to scrutiny.
Organizational
•  Executive requests for evidence that goes beyond satisfaction scores and completion rates.
•  Need to demonstrate a systematic approach to outcomes, not episodic wins.
•  Gap between having good outcomes and having a credible, decision-oriented narrative about why they happen.

5. Key Messages

Primary Message
The best-performing learning programs are not lucky. They evaluate differently. The research shows most organizations measure the wrong thing, and the ones that shift to measuring impact are the ones that can prove it.

Supporting Messages
•  InSync’s survey of L&D evaluation practitioners (N=200, 17 industries) documents an industry-wide gap between what organizations claim to measure and what they actually measure.
•  83% of practitioners feel their evaluation data gives an inaccurate or incomplete picture, the wrong question is being answered.
•  94% collect learner satisfaction data. Only 13% collect data relevant to business outcomes.
•  73% cannot articulate how management actually uses the evaluation data they report.
•  The 5 Shifts framework is the practical bridge: from “did they like it?” to “what will we change?”

6. Emotional Arc

Opening — Recognition. Open on the uncomfortable truth from the research: most organizations are collecting data that does not answer the business question. The reader recognizes their own dashboard.
Middle — Evidence. Walk through the survey data. Make the gap concrete with the numbers, then show what the exceptions do differently: they start from the organizational objective, not the data that is easy to collect.
Close — Conviction. By the close, the reader should feel that better outcomes are a matter of evaluation discipline, and that the shift is learnable. Point them to the research and the webinar as the next step.

7. Rational Proof Points & Data

PROOF POINTS REPLACED
The prior CB used InSync client case stats (400+ sessions, 30% confidence lift, 300+ courses, 35% engagement lift) as the evidence spine.
For Week 1 the evidence spine is now the Dye survey research, because that is the gated asset the CTAs drive to. Client case stats may be used as secondary social proof, but should not lead.

RESEARCH STATS (USE AS THE PRIMARY EVIDENCE)
•  83% of evaluation practitioners feel constrained by their data model, the data gives an inaccurate or incomplete assessment of the training.
•  73% could not articulate specific uses of evaluation data by management.
•  94% collect learner affective (satisfaction) response, the single most common data type collected.
•  Only 13% collect data relevant to business outcomes.
•  Only 24% integrate engagement, task-performance, or behavioral-change data. Only 14% use long-term learner outcome data.
•  54.5% use KPIs (Kirkpatrick or Phillips ROI) as their evaluation approach; weighted mean of 51.7% of deliveries evaluated.
•  81% of those who could name a use said the only use was resource justification, keep the program or cut it.
•  Base: N=200 practitioners who evaluate training at least quarterly, across 17 industries, plus N=24 interviews.

Case story / asset to reference: The Dye research report is the evidence spine. Frame the stats as an industry diagnosis, not as InSync self-promotion. The report ends on InSync’s decision-oriented, expertise-modified CIPP approach as the answer.
