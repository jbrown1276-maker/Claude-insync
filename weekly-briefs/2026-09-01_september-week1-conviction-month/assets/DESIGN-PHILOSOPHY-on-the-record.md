# On the Record — A Design Philosophy

*Aesthetic movement for the September "Conviction" chapter. Written to guide the visual expression of the InSync evaluation-research assets. The philosophy is the law; the stats, the hex, and the attribution are the fixed facts it must honor.*

---

## The Movement: Evidentiary Minimalism

**Communication through the artifact of record.** This is the aesthetic of the exhibit, the survey plate, the entered evidence — the quiet authority of a document that does not argue because it does not need to. A single number, set with the gravity of a monument, resting on a cited line. The composition behaves like a page from an imaginary discipline: the science of what organizations measure. Every mark is placed as though it will be examined under glass.

**Space as testimony.** Negative space is not emptiness here; it is the room a fact needs to be believed. The deep navy field is the surface of the record — vast, still, uninterrupted. Numbers breathe inside it. Nothing is crowded, because a crowded page reads as persuasion, and persuasion is the enemy of evidence. The margins are generous and exact, the vertical rhythm measured, the eye led downward through the composition the way one reads a finding: claim, contrast, citation, conclusion.

**Color as argument, rationed with discipline.** Three registers, never more. Muted gray is the ordinary — the easy data everyone collects, rendered without drama. Teal is the signal, spent only on what matters, so that its every appearance means *this is the thing.* Navy is the ground of proof. The contrast between the gray figure and the teal figure carries the entire argument before a word is read; the palette itself is the sentence. This restraint is the product of painstaking calibration — the kind of chromatic decision that looks inevitable only because it was labored over.

**The citation as a crafted object.** The source line is not a footnote apologizing at the bottom of the frame. It is a designed element — a fine teal rule, an exhibit tick, mono type set with the precision of a plate caption — bound to the number it certifies so the two read as one indivisible artifact. This binding of figure to source is the soul of the movement: evidence is a number *and* its provenance, made inseparable through master-level typographic care.

**Systematic marks, patient repetition.** Faint registration ticks, a whisper of baseline structure, the clinical apparatus of measurement — these accumulate quietly to suggest a discipline of observation without ever shouting. They reward the sustained gaze and vanish at a glance. The work should feel meticulously constructed, the result of countless refinements, as though a master spent hours deciding where a two-pixel rule begins and ends.

**Typography as evidence, not decoration.** The numeral is the hero and the largest gesture on the field; the interpretive line is a single quiet human sentence; the citation is the smallest, most exacting register. Sentence case throughout — the tone of a colleague stating a finding, never a headline shouting a claim. Nothing falls off the plate. Nothing overlaps. Every element sits within its margins with the composure of a piece that expects to be hung, examined, and trusted. This is craftsmanship at the top of the field: pristine, deliberate, and quietly undeniable.

## Fixed facts this philosophy must never bend
- Navy `#032B43` · Teal `#00B9F5` · Muted gray `#8A97A6` · off-white `#F4F1EA`. No fourth hue. No Golden Honey.
- Only verified figures appear: 94% (satisfaction, gray), 13% (business outcomes, teal — the lead), 83%, 73%. Never 24, "17 industries," or "14%."
- Every figure carries its source: "InSync 2026 industry survey of L&D evaluation practices, Dr. Charles Dye, N=200 + 24 interviews."
- InSync bug, white, quiet, never larger than the citation's importance.
- The 13% is given the last word.
