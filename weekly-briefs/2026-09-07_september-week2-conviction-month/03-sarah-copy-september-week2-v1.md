## 1. Weekly Newsletter

**Subject line options (sentence case)**
- **A (thesis / tension):** the number that impresses a room is rarely the one that survives the question
- **B (recognition):** you have stood in the room where someone asked how you knew
- **C (reframe):** claim exactly what you measured, and watch it hold

**Preview text (paired)**
- **A:** there is the impressive number and the defensible number. They are rarely the same one.
- **B:** the honest version of the story is the one that gets funded. Here is why.
- **C:** a number does not prove it will repeat. Find out whether yours will.

**Body**

There is the number that impresses a room, and there is the number that survives the follow-up question. They are rarely the same number.

Most of us have stood in front of an executive committee, named a result, and attributed it to a program. And most of us have privately known that three other things also happened that quarter. That is not dishonesty. It is compression, and the incentive rewards it. The trouble is the one question you cannot always answer when it comes. How do you know?

Here is the version that holds. In a healthcare technology program, confidence and proficiency rose 30%. That is what we measured, and that is what we will claim. We will not tell you it will rise 30% in your organization, because your conditions are not that program's conditions.

Read the second sentence again, because it is the credential. The limit is not an apology bolted onto the claim. It is the thing that makes the first number believable.

This is what outcome data can and cannot do, and the distinction is the whole game. Your data can show behavioral change, engagement, and application when you observe them directly. Your data cannot prove causation at scale without controls, and it cannot promise the same result next time under different conditions. Only 30 to 40% of learners apply what they learn without reinforcement, and application decays over time. (Saks and Belcort research on training transfer.) The conditions are not a footnote. They are the reason a result repeats or fades.

So before you claim an outcome will repeat, find out whether the conditions behind it will hold. The Live Learning Impact Diagnostic is a risk diagnostic, not a scorecard. You self-rate three areas, design, delivery, and continuity, as reliable, inconsistent, or at risk, and it shows you where your live learning runs on systems and where it runs on heroics, before scale makes the difference visible.
