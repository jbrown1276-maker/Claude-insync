## 1. Visual Concept Summary

"Exactly What It Shows." Week 1 put a number on the record and answered *who says so* with the source stamp. Week 2 answers the harder question the skeptical executive asks next: *so what does that actually prove?* The look is unchanged — deep-navy exhibit surface, one large numeral breathing, teal rationed to what matters — but the number now carries a second line of citation beneath the first. Where Week 1's stack read *number → source stamp*, Week 2's reads *number → source stamp → conditions bracket*: two lines at reading weight, bound to the number by a short teal spine, stating what the number measured (the CAN) and what it does not prove (the CANNOT), at equal weight, neither subordinate.

The whole week performs one behavior in the design: a client number never stands alone, not even for a beat. It arrives already conditioned. The conditions bracket is the visual embodiment of the week's one sentence — *a number always travels with its conditions, in the same unit* — and it is the single thing that makes an InSync stat visually distinct from every unconditioned case-study card in the feed. If "number plus conditions" does not read in under two seconds at a 150 px thumbnail, the differentiation is invisible and the asset has failed.

Two colors shift meaning to carry the new argument. Teal moves from marking *the payoff figure* to marking *the bond* between a number and its condition — it is now the spine that binds them. Muted gray moves from *the easy data everyone collects* to *the unconditioned number* — the naked claim, the Organization A version, a number missing something. One hard exception governs everything: a condition is never gray. Gray is only ever the color of a number stated without its conditions.

**No humans this week.** With the video cut, every asset stays human-free — evidence, not faces, exactly as in Week 1.

## 3. Typography System

Unchanged from Week 1. The three faces carry forward exactly. Week 2 adds one binding rule about which face carries the conditions bracket.

**Face 1 — Data / Display / Headline.** Inter or Archivo, tabular/lining figures. Hero numeral 180–320 px (down to ~64 px at thumbnail); headline 48–72 px; subhead 28–36 px. Numerals Bold/800; headlines Semibold/600; subheads Medium/500. The number is always the largest element.

**Face 2 — Interpretive voice (optional).** Source Serif or Newsreader for interpretive one-liners, 24–40 px, Regular/400–Medium/500. Never let the serif carry a number.

**Face 3 — The Source Stamp (mono / small-caps).** IBM Plex Mono or Space Mono, or Face 1 small-caps with wide tracking. 11–16 px (target ≥13 px on LinkedIn). Carries the source stamp ONLY.

**The conditions bracket uses Face 1 Medium at reading size — deliberately NOT Face 3.** The single most important typographic rule of the week. Mono reads as "quiet footnote," and the entire point is that the caveat is not a footnote. Face 1 Medium, sentence case, white/off-white, target 20–24 px at hero (never below the source-stamp size at any scale).

Sentence case throughout, including the conditions bracket. Tabular figures where numbers stack or compare.

## 4. Core Visual Motif — The Conditions Bracket

**What it is.** The one new structural element of Week 2, and a sibling to the source stamp — not a second competing motif. Week 1's source stamp answered *who says so*. Week 2 adds a second element answering *what does this actually prove*: two lines at reading weight, bound to the number by a short teal spine, stating the CAN and the CANNOT. The source stamp and the conditions bracket are **two lines of the same citation** — the source line (who) and the conditions line (what it proves). Together they are "exactly what it shows."

**The full stack, top to bottom, beneath any client number:**
1. **The number** — Face 1, hero scale, white (or teal only where teal is the single payoff moment).
2. **The source stamp** — Week 1 element, unchanged: thin teal rule (`#00B9F5`, 2–3 px hero / 1.5 px min thumbnail), 16–24 px below the numeral baseline, then a mono/small-caps citation line, quiet register. Answers *who*.
3. **The conditions bracket (NEW)** — a two-line pair at reading weight, bound by a short teal spine. Line 1 = **measured:** [what it measured] (the CAN). Line 2 = **does not prove:** [the limit] (the CANNOT).

**Geometric construction.** A short vertical **teal spine** (`#00B9F5`, 3 px hero / 2 px min thumbnail) at the left edge of the two condition lines, running the full height of both, tying them to the number's left edge above. It may terminate in a small tick/bracket foot to read as a bracket. Length = combined height of both condition lines; it must visibly enclose both, never just line 1. Line 1 (`measured:`) and Line 2 (`does not prove:`): labels + content in Face 1 Medium, white/off-white, 20–24 px hero, **same face, size, color, and weight.** Single line's leading between them, both enclosed by the spine. Gap from source stamp to bracket top: 20–28 px hero.

**States.**
1. **Full bracket** — number + source stamp + both condition lines, teal spine enclosing both. Default for every client stat asset.
2. **Comparison pair** — two numbers side by side: the unconditioned (Organization A) rendered gray with **no** bracket (the absence is the argument); the conditioned (Organization B) rendered white with the full teal-bound bracket.
3. ~~**Motion bracket**~~ — **OUT OF SCOPE this week (video cut).** Retain the spec in the Week 1/Week 2 record for future use if video returns.

**Non-negotiable rules.** Equal weight (the CANNOT line is never subordinate). Not the mono register (Face 1 Medium, never Face 3). White, never gray. Bound, not floated (the teal spine physically ties bracket to number). Two-second legible at 150 px (both condition lines survive). Reserved vertical space from the first sketch — the bracket is part of the number's footprint.

**Worked example (render exactly this language):**
> **30%**
> `— InSync client program, healthcare technology`  *(source stamp: mono, thin teal rule above)*
> │ **measured:** increase in confidence and proficiency  *(conditions bracket, white, Face 1 Medium, teal spine at left)*
> │ **does not prove:** it will repeat at the same rate in a different organization

**Never:** a checkmark/x, green/red, or pass/fail chart for CAN/CANNOT (turns a credential into a warning). A footnote (small, gray, mono, floated away). A second competing motif (badge, seal, ribbon, medallion). A subordinated CANNOT line.

**Scaling.** Hero: spine 3 px, condition lines 20–24 px. Thumbnail (~150 px): spine 2 px min, condition lines never below the source-stamp citation size, content may compress to the shortest faithful phrasing but **both lines must survive** — never drop the CANNOT to fit. Squint-test at 150 px; fix the numeral scale before sacrificing a condition line.

## 7. Layout Principles

1. The number gets the room — hero numeral always the largest element.
2. The source gets its fixed place — source stamp directly beneath every figure, never a footer.
3. **The conditions bracket gets reserved vertical space from the first sketch** — part of the number's footprint; never bolted on after.
4. The CTA gets teal, and teal gets the bond — teal marks the number-condition spine and the CTA, nothing else.
5. Completeness must read before the words do — squint-test: conditioned (white + bracket) vs unconditioned (gray, no bracket) reads as complete-vs-incomplete in under two seconds.
6. White space is a feature — min 80 px margins on hero; one idea per asset.
7. One primary CTA per asset — secondary actions are one-line text links.
8. Design for the save — self-contained, source and conditions baked in.
9. Thumbnail-first — validate at 150 px; both condition lines must survive.

## 8. What to Avoid

- Any client number without its conditions bracket (the behavior the week argues against; a naked "35% engagement lift" card would indict our own asset the moment the reader opens Blog 2).
- A condition rendered in gray, small, mono, or footnoted.
- The CANNOT line subordinate to the CAN line.
- CAN/CANNOT as checkmark/x, green/red, or any pass/fail iconography.
- A second competing motif (badge, seal, ribbon, medallion).
- Lifting Blog 2's hypothetical Org A/B numbers into any card; real internal client numbers inside the Wednesday comparison card (it is a labeled illustration).
- Re-running 94 vs 13 as a hero; rendering "confidence and proficiency" as "performance" or "ROI."
- Any human subject in any asset this week (video cut).
- Golden Honey `#eda113`, a fourth accent, fake dashboards, generic stock, red/alarm.
- 73% (either), the "60% decline" line, "17 industries," "14% long-term outcome data" — not rendered.
