# Lessons Learned — September Week 2 (Conviction Month · Proof Over Promises)

**Week:** Sep 7–12, 2026 · **Closed:** 2026-07-21 · **Scope:** Small team (Stages 1–3), full run from Stage 1, input = Jeff's 8-element brief + pre-written Blog 2

---

## What worked

**Eight strategic elements beat a stale CB.** Jeff deliberately withheld the old Week 2 creative brief (it had drifted) and supplied only Purpose, Audience, Weekly strategy, Goal, Pain point, Framework, Key messages, and Emotional arc. That was enough for Marcus to build a rigorous strategy, and it avoided importing stale assumptions (the retired "Readiness Strategy Tool," the wrong framework). Confirming the authoritative inputs up front is the fix for the Week 1 "two competing CBs" problem, and it held.

**The conditions bracket.** Devon's extension of the source stamp — measured / does not prove, bound to every client number at equal weight — gave the whole week one visual signature that is genuinely hard for a competitor to copy. It rendered cleanly at production (the comparison card and the 35% card both read in under two seconds). This is the strongest single design decision since the Week 1 colour-as-argument system and should carry into Weeks 3–4.

**Copy deck versioning paid off immediately.** The v1 (with video) → v2 (no video) split was tracked cleanly in the header and filename, and there was never confusion about which deck was current. The versioning rule adopted at the end of Week 1 earned its place in Week 2.

**Honesty theme executed without self-contradiction.** A week arguing that unconditioned numbers are not credible could easily have shipped a naked "35% engagement lift" card. It did not. Every client number carried its conditions in both copy and design.

## What needed revision

**The Diagnostic was mis-framed by two stages before we saw the source.** Both Marcus and Elena assumed the Live Learning Impact Diagnostic was a "what can you claim from your data" / measurement tool. When Jeff supplied the actual 202-page source, it turned out to be a **risk/reliability diagnostic** ("will your learning hold at scale? The Fade Effect") that explicitly is not a measurement framework. The correction was folded into Elena's brief and Sarah's CTA copy before Stage 3, so nothing shipped wrong, but the strategy and creative brief were both built on a wrong assumption.
→ **Change for next week:** get the actual description of the week's primary CTA asset (what it asks, what it returns) as a required intake item *before* Stage 1, not at Stage 3. A one-line accurate description of the destination is a Marcus input.

**Video cut late, after v1 was fully written.** The Leadership Video and 30-second script were dropped after Sarah had already produced v1 including scripted "pull-quotes" attributed to Karen and Jennifer. Those quotes were words the speakers never said on record. The no-video v2 had to strip every fabricated attribution.
→ **Change for next week:** confirm which assets are actually being produced before writing copy that puts on-record quotes in a named person's mouth. When a video's production status is unconfirmed (as it was all week), default to writing the copy that does not depend on it, and treat any speaker quote as provisional until the recording is confirmed.

**Blog 2 carried stat hazards that had to be caught by hand.** Two different 73% figures (Dye survey vs a Kirkpatrick-level stat), several external stats that had to be attributed to their own sources rather than the InSync survey, and a "60% decline since 2020" line that does not reconcile with the 17.4 → 13.7 figures in its own paragraph. All were flagged and kept out of short-form.
→ **Change for next week:** read the pre-written blog for stat hazards and internal arithmetic *before* briefing Marcus, and raise any in-blog error with the byline author during their review. The "60% decline" line still needs Jennifer's decision.

**Client stats with no source document, in the honesty week.** The 30% / 35% / 400+ figures came from Jeff's brief with no underlying measurement doc. Jeff approved their use, and the mitigation (state exactly what was measured, never inflate to performance/ROI) held. But it remains the week's structural tension.
→ **Watch:** if these numbers recur, get the measurement basis and window. A conditioned claim is only as good as the condition we can actually state.

**Devon's file-production is still a second pass.** As in Week 1, the approved design system was specs; a separate production step built the SVG/PNG files. Planned for, but it is now a reliable pattern and should be scheduled into the week from the start.
