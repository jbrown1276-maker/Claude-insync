## Copy Decisions

### Decision: Sarah's v1 copy rejected — v2 commissioned
**What happened:** Sarah's v1 copy used the same emotional entry point across all channels (practitioner in the training room). Jeff approved v2 after the orchestrator flagged the issue.
**What changed in v2:** Each channel opens on a different image or moment. The newsletter opens in the budget meeting. Tuesday LinkedIn opens with two words and silence. Wednesday opens with a falling number. Thursday opens with the CFO's specific concern. Emails open with the meeting that is coming.
**Carry forward:** Always brief Sarah on "different door for every channel." Do not let v1 patterns repeat in Weeks 3 or 4.

### Decision: Stats sourced by Sarah — not from the brief — confirmed by Jeff
**What happened:** Sarah's v2 used four stats not in the brief or blog source. Jeff confirmed all four as approved for use.
**Stats confirmed:**

- 3.7% spend decline, $101.8B → $98B — Training Orchestra / industry data

### Decision: 35% BizLibrary stat — email/newsletter long-form only
**What happened:** Alex flagged the BizLibrary stat as Medium-High credibility risk for a CLO/VP audience because BizLibrary is a vendor, not a research institution.
**Rule established:** The 35% BizLibrary stat lives in long-form email and newsletter body copy only, where the attribution is visible and readers can weigh it. It must not appear in paid ad headlines, LinkedIn post body copy, or stat cards. This is already the case in Sarah's v2 — no copy change needed, but the constraint must be honored in all future weeks if the stat is reused.

### Decision: "52% more likely" stat excluded
**What happened:** Karen Vieth's pre-written Blog 2 includes "companies actively tracking training ROI are 52% more likely to see their learning budgets increase" with no source citation.
**Rule established:** This stat is excluded from all campaign copy until a verifiable source is confirmed. Do not reintroduce it without sourcing.

### Decision: 40% proficiency stat rested in v2
**What happened:** Sarah's v1 leaned heavily on the "40% of talent development professionals rate themselves as proficient at evaluating impact" stat (ATD 2025). Sarah intentionally rested it in v2 to avoid repetition.
**Source confirmed:** ATD 2025. Available for reuse in Weeks 3 or 4 if appropriate.

### Decision: "Credibility risk" phrase — not used in v2

**Note:** This is strong, specific language from InSync's own CEO. Consider incorporating it in Week 3 or Week 4 copy when the credibility argument escalates. Flag to Sarah when relevant.

## Paid Media Decisions

### Decision: 3.7% stat — round for ad copy, precise for long-form
**Alex's recommendation:** In paid ad headlines, use "fell nearly 4%" rather than the precise "3.7%" when citing Training Orchestra data. The decimal precision looks overclaimed when the source is a vendor blog. In long-form email/newsletter copy, keep the precise figure with full attribution.

### Decision: Manual CPC bidding — no Smart Bidding
**Reason:** LinkedIn and Google Smart Bidding require ~50+ conversions in 30 days to optimize reliably. This is a first-run campaign without that history. Manual CPC with Enhanced CPC is the correct approach for Week 2 and likely Week 3.
