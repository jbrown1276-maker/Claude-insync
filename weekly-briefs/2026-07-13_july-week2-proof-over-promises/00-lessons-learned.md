## What Worked

### Register shift — practitioner to leader
The move from Week 1's practitioner register (Jennifer Lindsey Finan, facilitator language, inside-the-session moments) to Week 2's leader register (Karen Vieth, CFO conversations, budget room pressure) was clean and well-executed. The copy landed in the right emotional territory — quiet indictment, structural problem, not personal failure. This is the right arc to continue into Week 3 (systems lens) and Week 4 (direct invitation).

### Two-version discipline
Version A and Version B were properly treated as distinct entry points — not Version A with things removed. The newsletter, email, and Thursday LinkedIn each had a structurally different argument in the no-video version. Version B leaned on Karen's blog and the White Paper as authority anchors in their own right. This is the correct approach and must be maintained in Weeks 3 and 4.

### Jamie — no copy adaptations needed
Sarah's v2 copy was platform-ready for LinkedIn. Jamie made zero adaptations. This means Sarah's register and length calibration were correct for the channel. Good signal to carry forward: brief Sarah to continue writing LinkedIn posts at the same length and structural pattern.

### Alex's UTM architecture
Alex built a comprehensive UTM table covering every channel and asset. This is the right level of specificity for attribution. The naming convention (`proof-over-promises-w2`) should be continued as `proof-over-promises-w3`, `proof-over-promises-w4` for clean cross-week reporting.

### Casey's go/no-go gate structure
Casey's 15-item binary go/no-go gate is the right mechanism for a two-version campaign with multiple production dependencies. Continue using this structure for Weeks 3 and 4. The gate has teeth — and it should.

## What Needed Revision

### Sarah v1 — same entry door for every channel
Sarah's first draft used the same emotional opening across all channels. Every deliverable started from the same place — the practitioner in the training room. The orchestrator caught this before Jeff saw it and commissioned v2.

**What to brief differently in Week 3:** Explicitly instruct Sarah that each channel must open on a different image, question, or moment. The newsletter, the Tuesday LinkedIn post, the Wednesday post, the Thursday post, and the emails each need a distinct entry point. Include this instruction in the Sarah invocation prompt directly — don't assume Sarah will infer it.

### Stats came from outside the brief
Sarah sourced four stats not in the brief or source documents. The stats were strong and Jeff confirmed them, but this created a verification step that added friction. The brief's own stats (70%/48% LEO Learning, 3.4x ATD, 40% ATD) were not used in v2.

**What to brief differently in Week 3:** Before invoking Sarah, build a pre-approved stat library for the week — 4 to 6 stats with confirmed sources. Present them to Jeff for sign-off before Sarah sees them. This eliminates the post-copy verification step.

### Open production dependencies accumulated late
By Stage 5 (Casey), there were 12 open items — Leadership Video unconfirmed, paid budget unconfirmed, logo files unconfirmed, pixels not installed, UTMs not distributed. Most of these were knowable earlier in the pipeline.

**What to brief differently in Week 3:** Resolve the critical blockers (video status, budget, logo files, landing page pixels) before invoking Marcus, not after Casey flags them. Add a pre-Stage-1 checklist question to Jeff for each of these.

### "Credibility risk" phrase missed
Karen Vieth's Blog 2 uses the specific phrase "it creates credibility risk" to name the gap between activity metrics and executive credibility. Sarah didn't use this phrase in v2. It's strong, specific, and the client's own CEO language.

**Going forward:** When reviewing copy against source material, explicitly check for high-value phrases the source author uses that Sarah could carry into short-form copy. Add this to the statistics verification step.
