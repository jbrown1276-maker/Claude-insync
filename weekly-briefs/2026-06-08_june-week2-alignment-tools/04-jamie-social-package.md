## 1. Platform Strategy Summary

**LinkedIn — Primary and Only Platform This Week**

This is a B2B L&D audience. LinkedIn is where instructional designers, facilitators, producers, and L&D leaders spend professional time. It's the only platform warranting original content investment this week.

**Algorithm realities that affect execution:**

- **External links in post body** are penalized by LinkedIn's algorithm — posts with outbound links in the copy receive significantly less organic reach than posts with links in comments. All three posts already follow this correctly. Do not change this.
- **Native video** receives 3–5x more reach than posts linking to YouTube. Post 2 (Karen Vieth) must be uploaded directly to LinkedIn, not linked from YouTube. This is a production dependency.
- **Dwell time matters.** LinkedIn's algorithm rewards posts where users stop scrolling and read. Posts 1 and 3 are built for this. Post 2 is video-led — the thumbnail must create a stop moment.
- **Early engagement signals matter most.** The first 60–90 minutes of a post's life determine its distribution. Employee amplification should happen within this window.
- **Hashtags:** 3 is the sweet spot for this audience. More reads as broadcasting.
- **Character limit:** LinkedIn allows 3,000 characters. All three posts are well within that.

## 5. Engagement Response Guide

**General principle:** Respond like a practitioner who works with this material every day, not like a social media manager. Short, direct, specific. Never "great question!" Never "thanks for sharing!"

**Comment type: Practitioner recognition ("This is exactly what happens on our team")**
Response: *"What's the breakdown point you see most often? We built the Design Risk Scan specifically for that — the question on whether someone new could deliver reliably surfaces it almost every time."*

**Comment type: Questions about checklist content ("What does it actually cover?")**
Response: *"Three sections — Handoff Checklist, Run-of-Show Outline, and Design Risk Scan. The one most teams haven't done before is the Risk Scan: it ends with a risk level (Low/Medium/High) based on whether the program could be handed to a new delivery team and run reliably. Download link in the comments — worth 15 minutes before your next launch."*

**Comment type: Webinar questions ("When is it? How long?")**
Response: *"Registration link is in the comments on Thursday's post — date and time are on the registration page. It's designed as a working session — you leave with a breakdown points list and action plan specific to your programs."*

**Comment type: Skeptical ("We've tried checklists before. They don't stick.")**
Response: *"Fair. Most checklists fail because they're compliance documents, not conversation starters. This one ends with a pre-launch risk level — Low/Medium/High — which makes it a gate, not a form. If a program scores Medium or High, it doesn't launch until it's resolved. That changes the conversation with leadership."*

**Comment type: General InSync services inquiry →** Escalate to Jeff. Do not engage sales conversations independently.

**What to save/like/share:** Like all comments. Respond substantively to recognition, questions, and skepticism only — not every like. If a practitioner reshares with their own meaningful commentary, reshare with a one-line addition.
