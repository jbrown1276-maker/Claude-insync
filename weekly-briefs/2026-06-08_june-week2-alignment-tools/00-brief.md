## 2. Target Audience

**Primary Persona: Practitioners**
- Roles: Facilitators, Instructional Designers, Producers, Learning Operations Managers
- Pressure: Delivering consistent live learning across regions, time zones, and platforms while managing last-minute fixes and rework caused by unclear handoffs and misaligned design intent
- Emotional State: Frustrated by delivery breakdowns that feel avoidable, wanting a shared system rather than heroics, seeking practical tools they can use immediately
- What They Want: Clear frameworks, shared standards, practical workflows, and peer-validated approaches to reducing rework and improving consistency

**Secondary Persona: L&D Leadership**
- Roles: CLOs, Directors of Learning Operations, VPs of L&D, Senior Learning Leaders
- Pressure: Ensuring delivery quality scales reliably across regions and programs without adding headcount or escalating rework costs
- What They Want: Evidence that alignment between design and delivery produces measurable consistency improvements, and a clear path to operationalizing that alignment

## 4. Pain Point Framework

**Operational**
- No shared handoff document between design and delivery teams
- Run-of-show practices that vary by facilitator or region without a common standard
- Design risk not identified until the delivery team is already in prep week

**Emotional**
- Wanting a practical tool to take back to the team immediately — not a framework to study
- The relief of having a starting point that doesn't require building from scratch
- Feeling that alignment is always talked about but never operationalized in a usable format

**Organizational**
- Multiple programs running without a consistent handoff standard
- Design intent lost between the design phase and delivery briefing
- Teams spending delivery prep time on fixes that a shared risk scan would have caught upstream

## 7. Rational Proof Points & Data

- Teams with documented handoff standards and shared run-of-show practices report fewer escalations and smoother delivery prep
- Time-to-competency improves 25% and rework falls 30% when alignment frameworks are in place
- When design intent is made explicit before delivery, facilitators can tie session objectives directly to learner WIIFM — producing more durable behavior change
- A documented run-of-show reduces delivery variation across facilitators and regions by creating a shared execution standard
- Including a dedicated technical producer in virtual sessions can increase learner engagement scores by 30–40%
- Companies lose an estimated $13.5 million per 1,000 employees annually due to ineffective or absent training (Brandon Hall Group)
- 75% of organizations identify 'improving alignment between learning strategy and business goals' as their top L&D priority
