## 3. Google Search Strategy

### Campaign Structure

**Campaign name:** Proof Over Promises — W4 — Consultation
**Campaign type:** Search
**Goal:** Lead (consultation form submission)
**UTM campaign slug:** `proof-over-promises-w4`

### Bid Strategy

**Recommended: Manual CPC with Enhanced CPC enabled**

Smart Bidding (Target CPA, Maximize Conversions) requires a minimum of 30–50 conversions in the past 30 days to function reliably. We do not have that data. Deploying Smart Bidding on a fresh or thin conversion history hands the algorithm a problem it cannot solve — it will either over-spend on low-quality clicks or under-deliver to hit a phantom CPA target.

Manual CPC with Enhanced CPC gives us control over individual keyword bids while allowing Google to apply modest adjustments for higher-confidence auctions. When we accumulate conversion data across Q4, we can migrate to Target CPA.

**Daily budget:** $122 ÷ 5 days = $24.40/day
**Max CPC starting point:** $3.00–4.50 (adjust by keyword performance after Day 2)

### Keyword Plan

**Primary keyword group — Measurement intent (highest priority)**

| Keyword | Match Type | Rationale |
|---------|-----------|-----------|

| training effectiveness measurement | Phrase | Variant — same intent, different query structure |
| learning program evaluation | Phrase | Slightly broader but within ICP intent |

## 7. Stat and Claim Credibility Assessment

Alex reviews every claim appearing in Sarah's Week 4 copy for credibility risk. This is a blocking review — a challenged stat in a B2B practitioner audience does more damage than no stat.

### Claim 1 — Rater Drift (Casabianca, Lockwood & McCaffrey, 2015)
**Claim:** "Observer scores can fall from the 84th to the 43rd percentile over time, even when the actual performance being observed has not changed."
**In:** Wednesday InQuire carousel, Slide 5
**Assessment: APPROVED — use as written.**

This claim is verified against the primary study. Frontline Education's secondary citation is accurate. The inline attribution "Casabianca, Lockwood & McCaffrey, 2015" is the correct form for this audience. B2B practitioners will recognize an academic citation as credibility-building rather than excessive. Do not soften or qualify further.

**One note for paid ads:** This stat is appropriately placed in the carousel (an organic asset), not in Google Search ads or the LinkedIn boost copy. A stat this specific and academic does not belong in a 30-character headline. Keep it in the carousel where it has space to breathe.

### Claim 2 — Phillips ROI Model (concept reference)
**Claim:** "Programs that build that chain and capture what the room produces see returns that satisfaction scores and completion rates never account for."
**In:** Email #4 body
**Assessment: APPROVED — no credibility risk.**

No specific figure, no percentage, no decimal. This is a conceptual claim that a practitioner audience will recognize as directionally accurate from their own experience. The intentional absence of a fabricated ROI percentage is the right call. Approve as written.

### Claim 3 — False Confidence (concept reference)
**Claim:** "Most organizations are reading dashboards that say the program worked. The business outcomes often tell a different story."
**In:** Newsletter body
**Assessment: APPROVED — low credibility risk.**

Qualitative observation with no citable number. Standard B2B thought leadership register. A practitioner who pushes back has only their own experience to argue with, which means they are already engaged with the claim. This is the intended response. Approve.

### Claim 4 — Completion Rate Stat
**Status:** Does not appear in Week 4 copy. The 94% completion rate figure from Week 2 (Karen Vieth's blog) is correctly absent. No action required.

### Claim 5 — "Most programs" / "Most organizations" framing
**Assessment: LOW RISK — no action required, with one note.**

Qualitative framing is standard in this category. The risk is not credibility — it is the perception that the campaign is making sweeping claims without specifics. Week 4 has specific, verified stats (rater drift) appearing earlier in the arc. By Week 4, the audience has seen InSync's specificity. The "most programs" framing reads as earned generalization, not hand-waving.

**Watch for in paid ads:** Do not use "most programs" or "most organizations" as the hook in Google Search ad copy. Search ads need specificity in the description — use the consultation framing instead.

### Overall Credibility Risk: LOW
The Week 4 copy package is clean. One verified academic citation, two concept claims without figures, no fabricated stats. The approach of making conceptual arguments rather than unsupported specific claims is the right posture for a warm B2B audience that will scrutinize what it reads.
