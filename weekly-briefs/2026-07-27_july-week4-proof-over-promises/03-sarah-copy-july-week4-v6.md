### Newsletter Body

---

July built one argument. Most live learning programs are being asked to answer a question their measurement frameworks were never designed to answer. The evidence leaders need is not missing from the room. It is missing from the system. July showed where the gap lives, why it persists, and what a program built to close it actually looks like. This week is where it starts.

Not in the boardroom. Not in the dashboard. In the five minutes after a session ends.

The virtual classroom can go quiet fast. Participants log off. The producer closes the room. Chat is saved somewhere. And the facilitator — the person who just spent ninety minutes watching which moments landed, which scenarios produced confusion, which breakout group struggled with the same activity twice, each time for a different reason. Already thinking about the next calendar item. Without a place to put any of what they just saw.

That is where a lot of live learning proof gets lost.

Not because facilitators do not care. They usually care deeply. It gets lost because no operational habit exists to catch it. Most post-session routines were built for administration: export the chat, save the attendance report, send the follow-up email, flag the technical issue. Those steps matter. None of them answer the bigger question. None of them turn what a facilitator observed into evidence the learning system can use.

Cindy Foster names what that habit looks like.

One question. Asked before the next meeting takes over. "What did we observe today that tells us learners are more ready to perform than they were when they arrived?"

That question changes what happens in those five minutes. It moves the post-session conversation away from "Did we get through the content?" and toward "Did learners show signs of capability?" Those signs are specific. The participant who used the right language in a role play. The chat that shifted from basic clarification to application questions. From "how do I share my screen?" to "what does this look like in a compliance scenario?" The breakout group that made better decisions in the second round. Not because the instructions improved. Because the learners adjusted. That adjustment is evidence. Evidence of readiness building in real time.

It was in the room. It was visible. And without a habit for capturing it, it disappears before anything is done with it.

The InQuire Engagement Framework gives facilitators a practical lens for this work — not a form to fill out, but a set of questions worth holding: Were the conditions in the room supporting participation? Were learners thinking and applying, or completing and moving on? Were they engaged enough to lean in? Those three angles — environmental, intellectual, emotional — help facilitators turn what they saw and heard into something the program team can use.

A five-minute producer-facilitator debrief. One question before the evidence disappears. That is not extra work. It is the first link in an outcomes chain most live learning programs are missing.

The insight your facilitators carry out of every session is the evidence your organization needs to answer the question your executives are asking. The question is whether there is a path between them.

That path is what a consultation conversation is for. Not a pitch. Not a demo. A 30-minute call to apply the InSync diagnostic to your program. You bring your measurement questions. We bring the framework. No agenda other than what you are trying to answer and whether your current approach is built to answer it.

And so the evidence stays where it has always been. In the facilitator's head. Invisible to the program team. Never reaching the leader who needs it to answer a question about whether the program is working.

Cindy Foster writes about this moment with the specificity that only comes from having been there. She is not describing a general problem with post-session reflection. She is naming the exact operational gap between what facilitators observe and what ever makes it back into the learning system.

The participant who used the right language in a role play. The one signal that the concept actually landed. That it happened in a way that will hold. It was in the room. It was visible. And it disappeared before anything was done with it.

It is the difference between learners completing an activity and learners beginning to apply. It is observable. It is measurable. It is exactly what a post-session debrief is designed to surface.

The reader lived it. Let them recognize it.

The newsletter explores the post-session moment, the specific observable signals, the five-minute habit, and the InQuire lens — then arrives at InSync as the natural next sentence. The blog reference at the bottom is a separate section per the standing blog rule, but the newsletter body already lives inside Cindy's world. This is intentional.
