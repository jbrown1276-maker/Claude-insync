## 1. Visual Concept Summary

Week 4 is not a new statement. It is the resolution of three weeks of structured argument. The Q3 visual arc — structured incompleteness, a gap that opened in Week 1 and has been closing since — completes here. The line is whole. The motif is closed. The palette is settled. Visually, that means one thing above all others: this week's work should feel resolved, not complex. Fewer elements per frame. More white space. Cool Blue as the dominant accent — not the contrast it was in Week 3, but the fulfillment of an arc it was always building toward.

The concept behind the design is "The Path Between Rooms." The gap that existed between the facilitator's session and the leader's boardroom is now connected. The visual language does not show a gap or a break. It shows a line that is whole. That motif governs every asset: in the way dividers close rather than open, in the way the CTA block occupies its own visual space rather than competing for attention, in the way Cool Blue sits as a structural element rather than an accent trying to assert itself.

This is the earned close. The design should feel like it.

## 2. Color System

### Cool Blue — `#0883cf`
**Role:** Week 4 dominant signal color. The arc completes with Cool Blue. It is no longer an accent filling a gap — it IS the resolved state.
**Usage:** Structural dividers, CTA block backgrounds, accent rules, card backgrounds (quote card, carousel cover), horizontal rules in email and newsletter. Anywhere the visual needs to signal resolution and forward motion.
**Must never:** Share frame space with Golden Honey in any capacity. Never use as body text. Never reduce to hairline weight — if Cool Blue appears, it appears with structural weight.

### InSync Navy — `#032b43`
**Role:** Foundation anchor. The structure beneath everything. Week 4 is navy + Cool Blue. Navy holds. Cool Blue resolves.
**Usage:** Primary backgrounds on dark-theme assets (quote card, email header, newsletter header, carousel cover and CTA slide). All reversed-out (white) type lives on navy. The dominant background on any asset where Cool Blue is the accent.
**Must never:** Compete with Cool Blue for attention. Navy recedes; Cool Blue lands.

### White — `#ffffff`
**Role:** Resolution signal. Generous white space in Week 4 communicates completion, not absence.
**Usage:** Light-background assets (alternate quote card treatment, newsletter body, email body). Text on dark backgrounds. Breathing room between sections. The resolved state earns space — do not crowd it.
**Must never:** Be used as an accent. White is space, not signal.

### Slate Grey — `#6e8699`
**Role:** Supporting text. Body copy on light backgrounds. Captions and attribution text.
**Usage:** Subheads in newsletter body, body copy in carousel slides 2–4, attribution text on quote cards.
**Must never:** Appear in headlines. Never on dark backgrounds — use white instead.

### Light Blue — `#00b9f5`
**Role:** Energy accent, secondary to Cool Blue. Minimal use in Week 4 — the arc close does not need additional energy signals.
**Usage:** Permitted for a single accent within a carousel slide if a secondary accent is structurally necessary (e.g., an icon highlight within a dimension slide). Not a headline or CTA color this week.
**Must never:** Appear on the same visual plane as Cool Blue as a competing accent. If Light Blue appears, it is subordinate and contained.

### Golden Honey — `#eda113`
**RETIRED. Does not appear anywhere in Week 4.**
Not as an accent. Not as a border. Not as an icon fill. Not in a single pixel of any asset. A single golden element in Week 4 would signal Week 1 — a reset of the entire arc. This is a hard rule with no exceptions.

## 3. Typography System

### Montserrat Bold
**Usage:** All headlines across all assets. Quote card primary text. Carousel slide headlines. Email subject line visual treatment. CTA text in CTA blocks.
**Size range:**
- Hero headlines (quote card, newsletter header): 36–48px at 1200px canvas; scale proportionally
- Section headlines (carousel slides 1, 5, 6, 7): 28–34px
- Card/asset headlines (email header, newsletter header sub-line): 22–26px
**Weight:** Bold (700)
**Campaign-specific rule Week 4:** Headlines are short. One idea per headline. If a headline runs more than 10 words, cut it. The arc is over — the copy has arrived somewhere.

### Montserrat Medium
**Usage:** Subheads, promotional text, carousel dimension labels (Environmental / Intellectual / Emotional), callout blocks, the attribution line on the quote card when it needs more visual presence than Regular.
**Size range:** 16–22px depending on context
**Weight:** Medium (500)
**Campaign-specific rule:** Slide labels (the single-word dimension names) use Medium at 14–16px in uppercase. Not bold — Medium carries enough authority at uppercase treatment.

### Montserrat Regular
**Usage:** Body copy in carousel slides 2–4, newsletter body (delivered in email HTML), longer explanatory text anywhere it appears.
**Size range:** 14–16px for body; 12–13px for captions and fine print
**Weight:** Regular (400)
**Campaign-specific rule:** Shorter paragraphs than earlier weeks. Three sentences before a line break is the maximum. The writing has arrived — don't over-explain.

### Montserrat Light
**Usage:** Supplementary text only. Footer copy, fine print, secondary URLs where a lighter visual weight is appropriate.
**Weight:** Light (300)
**Must never:** Carry headline or CTA text. Light weight disappears in social compression.

### Open Sans
**Fallback only.** Used if Montserrat fails to load in email rendering environments. Do not spec Open Sans as a design choice.

## 4. Core Visual Motif — Week 4 State: Line Complete

### What it represents
The structured incompleteness motif — the gap that has defined the Q3 visual arc — closes here. Week 1 opened the gap with Golden Honey marking the break. Week 2 began closing it. Week 3 filled the gap with Cool Blue. Week 4: the line is whole. There is no break. There is no gap. The path is connected.

### Construction
The motif in its Week 4 state is a continuous horizontal or diagonal rule in Cool Blue `#0883cf`. No interruption. No gap point. No break in the line.

If used as a divider element: a single 2–4px rule in Cool Blue spanning the full width of the asset, with no gap or interruption. It separates sections — it does not signal a problem.

If used as an icon element: a closed loop, a complete arc, a connected path between two points. Two rooms connected by a line, not separated by a gap.

If rendered as an abstract motif in a header: the Cool Blue line completes across the full canvas. It does not leave white space at either end as a gap — it reaches both edges.

### What it must never look like
- A line with a break, gap, or open endpoint
- A partial circle or open arc
- Two separate elements that do not connect
- Any version of the motif from Weeks 1–3 with Golden Honey present

## 5. Photography and Imagery Direction

### Primary imagery concept: The Post-Session Moment
The visual moment Week 4 reaches for is after the session ends. The room has gone quiet. The facilitator is still in the space before the next meeting. The shared screen is empty. The chat window has gone still. This is the moment Cindy Foster's blog lives in — and it is the moment Devon's imagery should find.

This is not a moment of failure. It is the moment where evidence either gets captured or disappears. The visual tone is quiet, complete, and specific. Not dramatic. Not celebratory. Just present.

### Abstract imagery alternative
If photographic treatment is not available or not appropriate for a given asset, use abstract imagery of resolved forms:
- Complete lines (the motif in resolved state)
- Closed loops
- Connected paths between two points
- Whole structures — nothing fragmented or interrupted

### What to never use
- Generic stock photography of people in meetings or conference rooms — too generic for Week 4's earned register
- Stock imagery of dashboards, data visualizations, or analytics screens — InSync does not compete in the "better software" space and this imagery would suggest they do
- Any image with a visible gap, break, or disconnection — that is the Week 1 visual vocabulary, not Week 4
- High-contrast or high-tension lighting — the resolved state earns clean, direct light

### Lighting and mood
Clean, direct, without the high-contrast tension of earlier weeks. If there is shadow, it is soft. The visual mood is settled.

## 7. Layout Principles

1. **White space is the resolved state.** Week 4 earns more white space than earlier weeks because the arc is complete. Do not fill white space with additional elements. If a layout feels too spare, the layout is correct.

2. **One headline, one idea, one CTA per asset.** No asset this week should have two competing headlines or two CTAs. The CTA block is the most visually prominent element after the headline — it should be visually impossible to miss, and it should be singular.

3. **Cool Blue lands, it does not accent.** In Weeks 1–3, Cool Blue was filling a gap. In Week 4, it is complete. Use it with structural weight — as a background, as a rule that finishes rather than interrupts, as a CTA block that commands. Hairline Cool Blue accents undercut the arc resolution.

4. **Type hierarchy is strict on all carousel slides.** Label (dimension name) → Question → Body. The visual hierarchy makes the reader's eye move in that sequence without effort. If the body copy is visually competing with the question, the body is too large.

5. **The motif line (Cool Blue rule) completes the canvas.** On all dark-background assets, the Cool Blue horizontal rule runs the full width of the content area without interruption. This is the motif in its resolved state. It must not leave gaps at either end.

6. **The CTA graphic (Asset 5) is typographic, not illustrative.** "Book a consultation today." is the design. Do not add illustration, icons, or secondary imagery to this asset. The directness of the layout enacts the directness of the ask.

7. **Dimension slides (carousel 2–4) share a system.** The three dimension slides must feel like a family. Same grid, same type sizes, same position logic, different accent bar color only. A designer building Slide 4 from Slide 2 as a template should need to change exactly three things: the label color, the accent bar color, and the text content.

8. **Logo placement is consistent.** On all dark-background assets: horizontal white SVG, bottom-right, 80px from edges (64px on carousel slides). On all light-background assets where a logo is required: horizontal full-color SVG at the same position. On carousel slides: bug/mark white at 48px, bottom-right, 64px from edges. Never place the logo in the optical center of any asset.

## 8. What to Avoid

**Golden Honey `#eda113` in any form.** Not as a 1px rule. Not as an icon background. Not as a color in a gradient. One golden pixel would reset the Q3 arc to Week 1. This rule has no exceptions.

**Stat-led visual design.** The audience has been in this campaign for three weeks. An asset leading with a new statistic signals the campaign is starting over. Statistics appear on Slide 5 of the carousel as supporting evidence within a structured context — not as headline elements on any other asset.

**Re-explaining InQuire or the Live Learning Formula visually.** No framework diagrams. No three-pillar explainer graphics. These frameworks have been in the campaign. Week 4 references them — it does not re-introduce them.

**Dense, tight layouts.** A layout that feels full to the edges is a Week 1 layout — it signals there is a lot to say because the problem is new. Week 4 layouts have space because the argument is complete. If a layout needs to breathe, give it room.

**Two competing CTAs in a single asset.** Particularly on Asset 5 (Thursday CTA graphic): one CTA, one destination, one message. Any asset that carries both the consultation CTA and the White Paper CTA as equal visual elements is asking the viewer to make a choice that should have been made in the brief.

**Shadows, gradients, or texture used for tension.** Shadows and gradients work well for visual complexity and uncertainty. Week 4 has resolved. Use flat color blocks, clean rules, and generous white space instead.

**Generic LinkedIn engagement bait design elements.** No "SWIPE →" text overlaid on Slide 1 unless platform requirements demand it. No branded carousel-starter backgrounds with heavy pattern elements. The carousel's design earns the swipe through content quality, not visual gimmickry.
