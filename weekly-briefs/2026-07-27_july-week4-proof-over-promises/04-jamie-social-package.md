## 1. Platform Strategy Summary

**Platform in play: LinkedIn only.**

Week 4 is a three-post week on LinkedIn feed, plus one LinkedIn article (newsletter teaser). No other platforms are in scope this week. This is correct. The audience — L&D leaders and practitioners at the level of influence where consultation conversions happen — is on LinkedIn professionally. The campaign is four weeks in. This is not the week to test new platforms or experiment with reach. It is the week to convert a warm audience on the channel where that audience has been engaged.

**Algorithm and format considerations:**

LinkedIn currently deprioritizes posts with external links in the post body. This has been in effect long enough that it is campaign infrastructure, not a platform quirk. The standard mitigation — link in post body on Tuesday and Wednesday, first comment reinforcement — is appropriate for the practitioner-facing posts. Thursday is the exception: the consultation post is the direct conversion post, and the link stays in the body. The consultation URL is not a "click-through to content" ask — it is the action. The tradeoff (slight reach suppression) is worth the directness.

**PDF carousels are treated as native documents by LinkedIn's algorithm** — not external links — and consistently outperform image carousels in B2B niches on saves and shares. Wednesday's InQuire carousel is the highest-potential organic reach asset of the week.

**Optimal posting windows for this audience (L&D professionals, US):**
- Tuesday: 8:00–9:00 AM ET (before the first full-day meetings, practitioners checking feed before first session)
- Wednesday: 10:00–11:00 AM ET (mid-morning, decision-makers between calls)
- Thursday: 8:30–9:00 AM ET (leadership audience catching up before the day loads)

**LinkedIn Newsletter Teaser is a separate publish action.** It lives in the InSync LinkedIn newsletter (article format), not in the feed. It has its own audience and distribution logic. Treat it as a fourth content action this week with its own scheduling slot.

## 4. Hashtag Strategy

**Overall approach:**

The campaign uses a three-slot hashtag system. Slot 1 is always the post-specific audience tag (who this post is for). Slot 2 is always #LearningAndDevelopment (campaign-wide continuity, broadest reach, used every post all month). Slot 3 varies by post goal.

**Why three and not more:** This audience is professional and skeptical of over-tagged posts. More than three hashtags on a B2B LinkedIn post signals content that is optimizing for discovery over relevance. Three is the ceiling. For conversion posts (Thursday), the hashtags should feel like professional shorthand, not discoverability scaffolding.

**Tag selection by post:**

| Post | Slot 1 | Slot 2 | Slot 3 |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| Tuesday | #VirtualFacilitation | #LearningAndDevelopment | #LiveLearning |
| Wednesday | #VirtualLearning | #LearningAndDevelopment | #VILT |
| Thursday | #LearningLeadership | #LearningAndDevelopment | #TrainingROI |

**What to avoid:** Campaign-specific hashtags (e.g., #ProofOverPromises) on a brand account with this audience size. They have no existing search volume and they signal that InSync is trying to create a trend, which reads as self-promotional. Do not use them.

**Consistency note:** #LearningAndDevelopment has been used consistently across all four weeks of the campaign. The audience following along will see it as a campaign thread. Do not swap it for a synonym.

## 5. Engagement Response Guide

**Comments engaging with the argument (Tuesday or Wednesday):**

These are the highest-value comment type. When a facilitator or L&D professional responds to Tuesday's post with their own experience ("This is exactly what happens in our team"), the response is short and specific: acknowledge what they said, reflect it back with one sentence of elaboration, and leave the door open. Do not pivot immediately to the consultation CTA. Let the organic conversation run for 24–48 hours before adding a CTA-adjacent nudge ("If you want to look at how that gap works in your program specifically, we have a conversation starting point here: [consultation URL]").

**Questions about the White Paper (Wednesday):**

"How do I get it?" — The White Paper is available at insynctraining.com/why-go-live. No additional copy needed.

"Is this behind a form?" — Answer honestly. Do not avoid the question. "Yes, brief contact info to access. The paper itself is free."

**Questions about the consultation (Thursday):**

"What does that actually look like?" — Reply: "A 30-minute conversation. You bring your current measurement questions. We apply InSync's diagnostic to your program. No pitch. No slide deck. You leave with a clearer picture of whether your current approach is built to answer what you're being asked." Then link the consultation URL.

"Who would I be talking to?" — This should be answered by InSync's team, not in a public comment. Suggest they fill out the consultation form and note that InSync will be in touch to confirm the right person.

**Skeptical comments:**

"We already measure this" — Do not argue. "That is good to hear. The InQuire conversation is usually most useful for teams who have completion data and are being asked what it means. If that is not your situation, you are in a good place." Short. Non-defensive. If they engage further, engage further.

"This sounds like a sales call repackaged" — Acknowledge it directly. "That is a fair read. The frame we are working from is: 30 minutes, you bring your specific measurement questions, we do not have a product to sell you into, and if InSync is not the right fit for your program, we will tell you that. If it ever seems worth testing that claim, the form is here." Do not repeat this. One response is sufficient.

**What to save/like/share:**

- Save any comment that contains a specific program description, a named measurement challenge, or a question about InSync's framework. These are consultation leads in a pre-conversation state.
- Like all good-faith engagement on every post within 2 hours of posting.
- Share reposts or tags from practitioners within the feed — repost with a one-line acknowledgment. Do not share from L&D leaders without their explicit request or a very clear signal.

**When to escalate to Jeff:**

- Any comment from a named senior leader at a recognized brand
- Any comment that contains a complaint about InSync specifically
- Any comment that involves a media or press inquiry
- Any direct message requesting a consultation (forward to Jeff with context — do not respond on behalf of InSync)

**Handle independently:**

- All positive acknowledgments
- All "how do I get the White Paper" questions
- All general L&D conversation that doesn't name InSync specifically

## 8. Week 4 Close Signals for Future Q4 Planning

**What to watch and record by Friday August 1:**

1. **Consultation form submissions** — Total submissions attributed to the Week 4 social campaign (UTM-tracked). Anything above 2 is a strong close signal for a four-week B2B niche campaign. This data point calibrates how aggressive Q4's consultation framing can be from week 1.

2. **Tuesday saves** — If the practitioner post generates 25+ saves, Blog 4 resonated at a practitioner level beyond what we can see in comment engagement. This signals that Q4 should open with practitioner-facing content before moving to leadership conversion content.

3. **Wednesday carousel completion rate** — If full-read rate (all 7 slides) exceeds 40%, the InQuire framework at this depth has more audience interest than a surface-level overview. Consider a deeper carousel in Q4 (10–12 slides, full framework walkthrough) as a mid-campaign asset rather than a close-of-month asset.

4. **Thursday engagement-to-click ratio** — If engagement rate is high (4%+) but consultation clicks are low (<15), the audience is responding to the message but not converting. This is a CTA page problem, not a content problem. Flag to Jeff and InSync team before Q4 planning: the consultation landing page may need revision.

5. **LinkedIn Newsletter Teaser open rate** — If the newsletter open rate is below 15%, the subscriber list needs attention before Q4. A returning newsletter format after a one-week absence may also depress open rate for Week 4 — note whether the return gap (no newsletter in Week 3) shows up in Week 4 metrics as a benchmark for Q4 consistency decisions.

6. **Overall month pattern** — Record which week generated the highest consultation form submissions. If it is Week 4, the four-week warm-up to a direct ask is working and Q4 should replicate the architecture. If Week 3 or Week 2 generated more consultation intent (measured by form clicks even without submissions), the audience may be ready for the direct ask earlier than Week 4 in future campaigns.
