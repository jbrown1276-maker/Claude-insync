## 1. Project Overview

Week 1 opens June by establishing design as the upstream control point for delivery consistency. The core argument: when delivery fails, design usually shows up first. Blog 1 makes the cost of design-to-delivery drift visible across the verticals InSync serves — rework, delayed launches, compliance failures, inconsistent learner readiness. All content this week builds awareness and drives early webinar registration for **Design to Delivery Sync: Practitioner Collaboration for Consistent Live Learning.**

**Deliverables this week:**
- Blog 1 — Where Live Learning Delivery Is Won or Lost: Design Decisions
- Weekly Newsletter
- LinkedIn Posts (3 — T/W/TH)
- Email #1

**Primary CTA:** Register for the Practitioner Webinar

**Secondary CTA:** Preview the Design to Delivery Alignment Checklist (coming Week 2)

**Purpose:** Help facilitators, designers, and producers recognize that delivery inconsistency is a design problem — not a talent or effort problem. Build enough recognition and urgency to drive early webinar registration and tease the Alignment Checklist as the month's practical lead tool.

## 2. Target Audience

### Primary Persona: Practitioners
- **Roles:** Facilitators, Instructional Designers, Producers, Learning Operations Managers
- **Pressure:** Delivering consistent live learning across regions, time zones, and platforms while managing last-minute fixes and rework caused by unclear handoffs and misaligned design intent
- **Emotional State:** Frustrated by delivery breakdowns that feel avoidable, wanting a shared system rather than heroics, seeking practical tools they can use immediately
- **What They Want:** Clear frameworks, shared standards, practical workflows, and peer-validated approaches to reducing rework and improving consistency

### Secondary Persona: L&D Leadership
- **Roles:** CLOs, Directors of Learning Operations, VPs of L&D, Senior Learning Leaders
- **Pressure:** Ensuring delivery quality scales reliably across regions and programs without adding headcount or escalating rework costs
- **What They Want:** Evidence that alignment between design and delivery produces measurable consistency improvements, and a clear path to operationalizing that alignment

---

## 3. Week Strategic Goal

Establish the intellectual and operational case for why design decisions are the upstream control point for delivery quality. Make practitioners think "this is what's happening in our programs" while giving leaders the business cost framing they need to act.

This week does not pitch services or sell the audit. It builds recognition, names the cost, and earns attention through honesty about a problem the audience already feels.

---

## 4. Pain Point Framework

### Operational
- Facilitators and producers dealing with last-minute fixes caused by unclear design intent
- No documented handoff process between the design team and delivery team
- Delivery variation across regions that can't be explained by facilitator skill alone

### Emotional
- Frustration at fixing the same problems every program cycle without a systemic answer
- The exhaustion of heroic delivery when upstream design clarity would have prevented the crisis
- Feeling like quality is fragile and held together by individual effort rather than a shared system

### Organizational
- Rework and compliance remediation costs that stem from design-to-delivery gaps
- Delayed launches caused by design decisions that didn't account for delivery realities
- Inconsistent learner readiness across regions because design intent wasn't shared with facilitators

---

## 5. Key Messages

**Primary Message:** When delivery fails, design usually shows up first. The gap between what was designed and what was delivered is where quality is won or lost.

**Supporting Messages:**
- Design decisions are the upstream control point for facilitator consistency, producer stability, and learner readiness
- The cost of design-to-delivery drift is visible in rework, delayed launches, compliance remediation, and inconsistent outcomes across regions
- This is a systems problem, not a talent or effort problem — and it has a practical solution
- June's practitioner webinar gives teams the workflows to close the gap — register now to secure a seat
- The Design to Delivery Alignment Checklist — coming next week — is the practical starting point for every program

---

## 6. Emotional Arc

**Opening — Recognition:** Open with the familiar pattern practitioners know immediately: strong content, great facilitator, inconsistent delivery across regions. Name it before diagnosing it.

**Middle — Diagnosis:** Show that the root cause isn't talent or effort — it's design decisions that didn't account for delivery realities. Make the cost concrete with vertical-specific examples.

**Close — Relief:** The webinar is where this problem gets solved with real workflows and peer case stories. Tease the Alignment Checklist as the practical tool arriving next week.

## 7. Rational Proof Points & Data

- Rework and last-minute fixes are the most visible cost of design-to-delivery drift — teams report spending 20–30% of delivery prep time correcting upstream design gaps
- Time-to-competency improves 25% and rework falls 30% when design-to-delivery alignment frameworks are enforced
- Inconsistent learner readiness across regions is almost always traceable to design intent that was never made explicit for the delivery team
- Organizations with strong learning design and facilitation systems achieve higher performance and stronger retention

**Case Story / Asset to Reference:** Use vertical-specific failure examples in Blog 1: tech (training lags product cycles), manufacturing (delivery variation affects safety), healthcare (clinical inconsistency from facilitation drift), energy (field risk from unclear run-of-show), financial services (compliance rework from design gaps).
