## 1. CAMPAIGN VISUAL SYSTEM

### Color Usage Rules — Campaign-Specific

**InSync Blue #032b43** — The ground. Every social graphic and email header lives on this. Use as the dominant background for all dark-ground surfaces. Never use as a text color.

**Medium Blue #044269** — Structural containers inside the primary field — content boxes, secondary panels, the "Design Intent" state in the gap diagram.

**Cool Blue #0883cf** — Motion, flow, the arrow that's trying to connect. Use on directional elements (arrows, connectors), progress indicators, and the aligned bridge state (Thursday only). One per visual maximum when used as a solid shape.

**Light Blue #00b9f5** — Accent energy. Thin rule lines, icon outlines, data callout borders. Never fill a large area with this.

**Slate Grey #6e8699** — Secondary text only — captions, labels, metadata, attribution lines.

**Golden Honey #eda113** — ONE use per visual. Hard stop. Reserved exclusively for the key qualitative reframe moment. No percentages this week. If you're tempted to use it twice, you've found your signal that the visual has too many ideas.

**Ivory Mist #d9d4c5** — The gap itself. In every diagram, the space between Design Intent and Delivery Reality is Ivory Mist. Should feel like something that should have been filled and wasn't. Also used as breathing room in newsletter and email layouts — wide interior margins, inter-section space. Never use as a text background.

**Aqua Sky #3ba7bf + Ocean Blue #0f698c** — Iconography and callout elements only. No headlines, no backgrounds, no diagrams.

### Spacing and Grid Principles

**Social Graphics (1080×1080px and 1200×628px):**
- Outer margin: 64px on all sides
- No content crosses into the outer margin
- Internal padding on any container: 32px minimum
- Single-column layout. No multi-column on social.

**Newsletter and Email (600px wide):**
- Left/right outer margin: 40px
- Content column: 520px
- Section dividers: 40px Ivory Mist vertical space between sections
- Internal padding inside content blocks: 24px horizontal, 20px vertical
- Image/diagram blocks: full content column width (520px), with 24px margin above and below

**Gap Diagram — Zone Proportions:**
Approximately 38% / 24% / 38% (Design Intent / Gap / Delivery Reality). The gap zone is NEVER labeled with a word. Its emptiness is the label.

---

### "One Idea Per Visual" — Audit Test

Before finalizing any asset, ask:
1. What is the single sentence this visual is arguing? If you need more than one sentence, it has too many ideas.
2. Where does Golden Honey appear? If it appears twice, remove the idea that matters less.
3. Can a practitioner understand the argument in under 4 seconds?
4. Is there anything in the frame that does not serve the single sentence from question 1? Remove it.

When a visual fails the test: delete before you edit. Subtraction is faster than refinement.

## 3. PHOTOGRAPHY ART DIRECTION

*Photography is not in scope this week — all five assets are diagram-and-type led. Use this direction if extending the campaign to Week 2 or if a strong stock image is found.*

**Shot:** Two people in a handoff moment. One person handing a document to another, or reviewing a document just marked up. Key gesture: moment of noticing — a pause, eyes on the page, a finger pointing to a passage. Not a warm exchange. Not agreement.

**Talent:** One reads as a designer/instructional designer (print materials, tablet, margin-noted document). Other reads as a facilitator/producer (the receiver in the handoff). Diverse casting required. Business casual to corporate casual.

**Setting:** Modern office or home office. Cool natural light from one side (5500–6500K equivalent). No lifestyle warmth, no decorative plants. Home office should look like a serious working environment, not a coffee shop.

**Action:** Reading, marking up, reviewing. No smiling, no handshakes, no eye contact with camera, no resolution gestures.

**Framing:** Horizontal crop for newsletter/email headers (600×360px, subjects in center-left two-thirds). Square or horizontal crop for LinkedIn (subjects in bottom 40–60% of frame, headline above on dark overlay).

**Reject in stock search:** Smiling subjects, open laptops with presentations, learners at keyboards, polished training rooms, warm lighting, subjects making eye contact with each other.

## 5. DESIGN RATIONALE

**The Gap Diagram**
A simple horizontal systems diagram makes the coexistence of both states visible without editorializing. It doesn't say the design is bad or the facilitator is wrong — it shows that the connection was interrupted. That structural neutrality is what makes practitioners recognize themselves in it. A more expressive visual form would attribute blame. The diagram attributes a gap.

**Ivory Mist for the Gap**
Ivory Mist is the one warm color in an otherwise cool palette. That warmth makes it slightly uncomfortable against the blues — it reads as absence that shouldn't be there. If the gap were white, it would read as breathing room. If it were dark, it would read as a barrier. Ivory Mist reads as something that should have been filled, which is exactly the feeling we need practitioners to have.

**The Broken Arrow**
An arrow that stops mid-gap says: this connection was attempted and failed. No arrow would say: no one tried. The broken arrow implicates the gap, not the sender — we're not saying practitioners aren't trying. We're saying the gap defeats the effort.

**InSync Blue as Dominant Background**
This campaign makes a claim to authority. A lighter background reads as educational content. A dark ground reads as a perspective worth engaging. The choice is rhetorical as much as aesthetic.

**Golden Honey Once Per Visual**
Golden Honey is warm in a cold palette, saturated in a restrained palette, and bright against dark grounds. If it appears twice, the eye splits its attention and the hierarchy collapses. Reserved for one line — the qualitative cost of the gap — it functions as visual punctuation. The color says: this is the thing worth pausing on.

**No Photography This Week**
Photography introduces specificity the campaign, in its first week, can't afford. We need practitioners to say "that is us" — and photography too quickly makes the image about two specific people. The diagram-and-type approach keeps the argument at the level of systems, where recognition operates when asking someone to see their own practice clearly.
