## 1. PLATFORM STRATEGY

### Why LinkedIn Is the Only Primary Platform This Week

This week's audience — Facilitators, Instructional Designers, Producers, Learning Operations Managers, CLOs, and L&D Directors — lives on LinkedIn in a specific and useful way. They scroll during work hours, they save content that helps them articulate a problem they've been carrying, and they share posts that make them look informed to peers and leadership. That's precisely the content this campaign produces.

More importantly, the emotional arc of this week (Recognition → Diagnosis → Relief) requires three sequential posts that build on each other. LinkedIn's feed algorithm rewards content that earns early saves and dwell time, which pushes subsequent posts from the same account to the same audience. Tuesday's post primes the audience. Wednesday deepens it. Thursday converts it. That sequence only works on a platform where professional identity and continuous scrolling intersect — which is LinkedIn.

The word "practitioner" in the webinar title is doing real positioning work. On LinkedIn, that word signals peer-to-peer professional content rather than vendor content. That signal travels. It does not travel the same way on any other platform.

**Do not activate secondary platforms this week.** Reasoning by platform:

- **Instagram:** InSync's audience doesn't consume L&D thought leadership here. The visual system Devon designed will look right on LinkedIn and wrong on Instagram, where the dominant register is aspirational lifestyle or bold graphic hooks with personality. Activating Instagram this week dilutes production focus without measurable return.
- **X/Twitter:** The L&D community still exists on X, but engagement has fragmented significantly and the platform cannot support the long-form argument format Sarah wrote. Chopping a 130-word post into X format loses everything that makes this copy work — the operational specificity, the paragraph rhythm, the build.
- **Facebook:** Not this audience, not this content type.

**Secondary platform to watch for Week 2:** If LinkedIn engagement data shows strong comment volume from L&D Directors and CLOs, consider a LinkedIn Showcase Page post or LinkedIn Newsletter teaser in Week 2 when the Checklist arrives. That's a different content type — downloadable asset, clear CTA — that maps better to LinkedIn's document format.

## 6. ENGAGEMENT STRATEGY

### How to Respond to Comments

Every reply should read like it came from a practitioner, not a brand account.

**Recognition comments ("This is exactly my Tuesday"):**
Reply with specificity — mirror their language, don't validate generically. Example: "The run-of-show version of this is particularly sharp — producer finds out about the configuration in the briefing call, not the design doc." Short, peer-level, specific.

**Forward-leaning comments ("What's the solution?"):**
Point to Thursday's webinar (if pre-Thursday) or the Checklist (if post-webinar). Don't pitch — offer: "We're building the working answer to that in the June 25 session — registration is in the first comment if you want to be in the room."

**Disagreement or pushback:**
Engage it. This audience respects intellectual honesty more than brand smoothness. If someone argues the problem is talent not systems, engage the distinction directly. This is a signal the post is reaching leadership, not just practitioners.

**Peer tagging / shares:**
Like the comment immediately. If someone tags a colleague, reply to welcome the new person: "Good timing — [tagged person], we're running the working session on June 25 for exactly this context."

**Response cadence:** Check comments at 30 minutes post-publish, again at 2 hours, again at end of day. Thursday's post warrants a check every 30 minutes through noon given same-day webinar urgency.

### What to Monitor

- **Save rate** (Tuesday and Wednesday primary metric) — visible in LinkedIn Creator Analytics. Saves indicate the post is landing as reference-worthy content.
- **Share-to-like ratio** (Wednesday primary) — shares indicate the "systems not talent" reframe is traveling beyond InSync's existing audience.
- **Link clicks in first comment** (Thursday primary) — use a UTM-tagged link for the webinar registration URL to track clicks from social.
- **Profile visits** — spike after Tuesday post indicates discovery (new audience finding InSync for the first time).
- **Follower growth** — any uptick this week indicates content is reaching outside the existing network.

### Amplification Trigger

A post is performing if, within the first 2 hours:
- Tuesday: 15+ reactions and 5+ saves (saves are weighted heavily in LinkedIn's algorithm)
- Wednesday: 5+ comments (especially comments that extend the argument or share a peer's version)
- Thursday: 10+ link clicks in first comment (via UTM) and registrations correlating in HubSpot

If Tuesday hits these thresholds at 2 hours, consider boosting via LinkedIn Campaign Manager as Sponsored Content (flag to Alex). Do not spend before the 2-hour check — you need organic signal first.

### Poor-Performance Pivot

*Poor signal:* Fewer than 10 reactions at 2 hours, zero comments, no saves. Usually means the hook didn't survive the "see more" cut.

*Pivot options:*
- Re-post at a different time (Thursday 1 PM for Tuesday's post if it stalled) — LinkedIn allows this without penalty if the original post is deleted first. Only do this if Tuesday's post genuinely fails.
- On Wednesday: if Tuesday was weak, open Wednesday's post with a direct address — "I want to come back to something from Tuesday." This manually bridges the posts for anyone who missed the first one.
- On Thursday: if Wednesday was weak but Tuesday was strong, the conversion post still works — the Tuesday saves mean that audience will see Thursday's post. Don't abandon the sequence.
