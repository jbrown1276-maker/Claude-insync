## 1. Visual Concept Summary

Week 2 is the pivot from naming the gap to showing the path through it. The campaign argument — that the evidence already exists and what's missing is the translation step — lands visually as motion, not as arrival. Last week (August Week 1), the arc motif line ran to 78% resolved and stopped. This week, it advances further. The gap is visibly narrower. Cool Blue is not an accent anymore — it is building. The feeling is: something is in motion, not stuck. Not yet complete, but demonstrably closer.

The visual register shifts from diagnostic precision (Week 1: clinical, cold, the instrument naming the problem) to evidential confidence (Week 2: the instrument showing results beginning to arrive). The layout opens slightly — more white space, less architectural density. The data is still the point. But now the data is moving.

Every asset this week answers the reader's implicit question from Week 1: what do I do with this gap? The translation path is the visual answer. Assets show the journey from session-level observation (left) to organizational evidence (right). The gap is narrowing. Cool Blue marks where evidence is arriving.

## 3. Typography System

**All type is set in Montserrat. Fallback stack: `'Montserrat', 'Open Sans', Arial, sans-serif`.**

| Variant | Usage | Size Range | Weight | Campaign-Specific Rules |
|---------|-------|------------|--------|------------------------|
| Montserrat Bold | Primary headline, stat numbers | 36–64px (scaled to canvas) | 700 | One Bold headline per asset — no exceptions. The headline is the single visual anchor. Nothing else competes with it at the same scale. Sentence case throughout. No all-caps in Bold. |
| Montserrat Medium | Subheads, stat context labels, attribution headers, section labels | 16–28px | 500 | Supports the Bold headline — never competes. Title case for stat labels only ("Evidence Collected," "Programs Measured"). Sentence case everywhere else. |
| Montserrat Regular | Body copy, source attribution, CTA labels, metadata | 12–18px | 400 | CTA labels: 13–14px, all caps, letter-spacing +50. Source attribution: 13–14px, Slate Grey. Max 65 characters per line in body blocks. |

**Hierarchy principle:** The size contrast between the Bold headline and everything else must be significant — minimum 2:1 ratio. If the headline doesn't dominate, the layout is wrong. The reader's eye should never have to choose between two equally weighted elements.

**Campaign-specific typographic rules:**
- Headers advance the argument — "What executives actually want from your data" not "Understanding executive measurement needs." If the headline could appear in a vendor brochure, rewrite it.
- Numbers are always Bold, at the same size or larger than any accompanying label.
- No italic — this campaign is evidence-forward, not narrative.
- No decorative treatment around the headline — the type is the element.
- Sentence case is the rule. The only all-caps text is CTA labels and, where noted, stat source labels in small attribution text.

## 4. Core Visual Motif — The Translation Path, Gap Closing

**What it represents:** The journey from session-level observation (raw practitioner feedback, facilitation notes, behavior indicators — left side) to organizational evidence (executive-level reporting, performance data, business outcomes — right side). Each week of this campaign, the gap closes further. Week 2 position: visibly narrower gap than Week 1. The line is not complete — completion is Week 4. But motion is visible and specific.

**Week 2 motif state:** The arc line is approximately 85–88% resolved. In Week 1, the line ran to 78% and stopped. This week, it advances to approximately 88% before the terminal gap. The remaining 12–15% is the translation step still being built — visible absence, not forgotten absence.

**Construction — two variants for this week:**

**Variant A — Gap Closing (primary: blog hero, newsletter header, email header)**
- Single horizontal rule, full available canvas width
- Left segment: Navy-to-Teal gradient bar OR solid white at low opacity (25%), runs from left margin to the motif's gap point
- The gap: 10–12% of total usable width, absent (transparent against Navy background). The gap represents the translation step not yet taken.
- Right segment: Cool Blue `#0883cf`, solid, runs from gap end to right edge. 28–32% of total width. This is evidence arriving.
- Stroke weight: 3px at 1200px scale / 2px at 600px scale. Hard, flat terminations — no rounded ends, no arrowheads, no gradient fade at the endpoints.
- Position: Bottom 20–25% of canvas, below all headline and body content, above logo.
- Gap endpoint markers: Two dots, radius 4px, Cool Blue `#0883cf`, placed at each edge of the gap. These are the only decoration permitted on the motif.

**Variant B — Translation Path as Horizontal Flow (LinkedIn square assets)**
In square format (1200×1200px), the translation path runs as a thinner structural element — not the dominant motif but present as an organizing line. It sits at 82–85% vertical height, same construction as Variant A but proportionally adjusted to the square canvas. Width: 1040px usable (80px margins each side). The gap is present but the path does not need to be the focal element — the stat or concept phrase is. The motif earns its place as structural support.

**SVG pattern for all Week 2 gap instances:**

**What the motif must never look like:**
- A progress bar — no rounded ends, no percentage label, no completion indicator text
- A decorative divider between sections — it is a semantic element, not a separator. Do not place it between two text blocks as a rule.
- A loading indicator — it is static. No animation in any static asset.
- Any color other than the specified white-fade (left) and Cool Blue (right) — no Teal, no Ocean Blue, no gradient bridging the gap
- A "before and after" split that requires a label to explain it — the motif should read as in-motion without a legend

## 5. Photography and Imagery Direction

**This campaign does not use photography in social or header assets.** These are typographic and data-visualization assets. No photographs on LinkedIn posts, newsletter headers, email headers, or blog hero.

**If a long-form blog or article requires a featured image** (beyond the blog hero SVG), the direction is:

**Scenario:** Abstract data in translation — not a dashboard with fake numbers, but the visual grammar of a system moving information from one state to another. Flows, signal paths, the infrastructure of measurement made visible.

**Lighting and temperature:** Cool, blue-toned, clinical. No warm light, no golden-hour palette, no amber tones of any kind. Blue-white contrast only.

**If human subjects appear:** They are in specific observation or documentation — looking at a readout, writing into a framework, at a workstation with data visible. They are not the subject. The system they interact with is. No eye contact with the lens. No group shots. No smiling.

**Abstract alternatives (strongly preferred):** Geometric data visualization in partial completion states. Systems diagrams with some connections present and some visibly absent. Session quality readouts. These are preferred over photography because they hold the temperature and register of the campaign without risk of wrong emotional tone.

**Never use:**
- Training room photographs of any kind
- Facilitators in front of whiteboards or screens
- Generic conference-room or boardroom stock
- Smiling executives
- Handshakes or any "partnership" visual cliché
- Any image readable as "corporate learning content"
- Images with visible warm/amber tones — they contaminate the Week 2 cool, evidential register
- Illustrated characters or icon-people of any style

## 7. Layout Principles

1. **One Bold element per asset, no exceptions.** The primary headline or stat number is the single visual anchor. If two elements compete at the same scale and weight, the asset has no hierarchy and no argument. Identify the one thing the reader reads first and make everything else subordinate to it.

2. **White space is structural — not a gap to fill.** Every asset has a mandatory breathing margin: 80px minimum on 1200px canvases, 32px minimum on 600px canvases. These margins are not negotiable under deadline pressure. An asset with tight margins reads as anxious. This campaign reads as confident.

3. **The arc motif earns its position.** It sits at the bottom of the canvas, below all headline and body content, above the logo zone. It never appears above a headline, between two text blocks, or centered in the canvas as a decorative focal point. It structures the bottom of the asset — separating content from the logo anchor.

4. **Cool Blue is a signal, not a backdrop.** When it appears, something is either arriving (evidence) or actionable (CTA). LinkedIn Post 2 is the single exception where Cool Blue is the background — that exception is specific to the stat card and does not transfer to other assets.

5. **Left-align all headlines and body text.** Center alignment is reserved for stat cards where the number is the entire design (LinkedIn Posts 2 and 3). Every other asset: left-aligned, anchored to the left margin constant.

6. **Stat cards are standalone assets.** They must read at social card thumbnail scale without explanation. One stat, one source attribution, one visual accent. If a stat card has more than four distinct text elements, it has too many. The number is the design.

7. **The logo occupies the bottom-left of every asset.** The exception is stat cards where the CTA direction text competes — in that case, logo goes bottom-left and CTA text is centered above the arc motif. Logo is always white horizontal on dark backgrounds. Logo is always inline SVG paths — no external file references in production SVGs.

8. **No warm contamination at any scale.** Teal `#2E7D8C` is the warmest color that appears in this campaign, and it appears sparingly (email header only, as a single vertical accent). Nothing warmer appears. Check every asset at export — any amber, orange, or warm-grade element is a production error.

## 8. What to Avoid

**Golden Honey (#eda113) in any form.** This has been stated in every section and is restated here. It is retired. It does not appear at any scale, in any element, in any asset, for any reason. Production should flag any hex value starting with #ed, #e8, #d9, #f0 that reads as warm — those proximity values can creep in on gradients.

**Gradient bridging the arc motif gap.** The gap in the translation path motif is a clean break. No gradient fades, no soft transitions, no "almost connected" visual blur. Hard edges at the gap endpoints. The gap is the point — it must be visible and unambiguous.

**Rounded ends on the arc motif bars.** `stroke-linecap` is always `square`. Rounded ends read as a progress bar or a UI loading element. The arc motif is not interactive. It is a structural diagnostic — hard, flat terminations.

**More than two Cool Blue instances per asset.** Each instance must be purposeful. Ask what the Cool Blue use signals before placing it. If the answer is "visual interest" or "balance," remove it.

**Centered headlines outside of stat cards.** Centering signals uncertainty — the headline doesn't know which side to commit to. Left-alignment signals conviction.

**Body copy blocks on LinkedIn assets.** The LinkedIn posts are concept-phrase, stat-card, and authority-close assets. They do not carry body copy. If text requires more than two supporting lines, it belongs in the caption, not the asset.

**Templates that recycle across all three LinkedIn posts.** The three posts share a design system — same Navy background (Posts 1 and 3), same logo placement, same motif positioning — but they are not the same template with swapped text.
