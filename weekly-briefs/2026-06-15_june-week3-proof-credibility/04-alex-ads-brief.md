## 1. Channel Strategy and Budget Split

**Recommended channel mix: LinkedIn Ads (primary) + Google Search (secondary)**

Week 3's objective is webinar registration conversion. The audience — L&D practitioners and leadership — is most reachable and most conversion-ready on LinkedIn. Google Search captures the high-intent practitioner who is actively researching design-to-delivery alignment at this moment. Display is not recommended for Week 3; the audience is too niche for efficient display targeting, and the budget is better concentrated where signal quality is highest.

| Channel | Strategic Role | Recommended Budget % | Estimated Spend Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn Ads | Primary: audience-matched conversion + retargeting | 70% | $1,400–$2,100/week |
| Google Search | Secondary: high-intent keyword capture | 30% | $600–$900/week |
| Display/Retargeting | Not recommended Week 3 | 0% | — |

**Total recommended weekly budget: $2,000–$3,000**

This is a niche B2B audience. Below $2,000/week, LinkedIn volume will be insufficient to generate meaningful conversion signal within a single week. The floor for LinkedIn alone to drive webinar registrations at scale is approximately $1,200–$1,500/week with tight audience targeting. Below that floor, results are anecdotal, not actionable.

If the budget is below $2,000: concentrate 100% on LinkedIn. Drop Google Search entirely rather than splitting a sub-floor budget across both channels.

## 2. LinkedIn Paid Strategy

### How Week 3 Differs from Week 2

Week 2 LinkedIn ads were (or should have been) checklist-download focused — problem-diagnostic creative pointing to a tangible tool. Week 3 creative shifts entirely to proof-forward, webinar-conversion creative. The audience segment targeting also evolves: Week 2 needed broad practitioner reach for checklist awareness. Week 3 retargets the audience that has already engaged (checklist downloaders, blog visitors) and adds a cold practitioner audience using the proof argument as the hook.

### Audience Targeting

**Retargeting segment (highest priority — warmest audience):**
- Website visitors from Blog 1 and Blog 2 (past 30 days)
- Checklist downloaders (matched via email list upload or landing page visitor pixel, past 30 days)
- LinkedIn post engagers from Weeks 1 and 2 (reactions, comments, shares, saves)
- Estimated segment size: depends on site traffic and list size — if under 1,000 matched users, LinkedIn retargeting will be limited. Flag to Jeff if site traffic has been low.

**Cold practitioner audience (second priority):**
- Job titles: Instructional Designer, Facilitator, Learning Operations Manager, Training Producer, L&D Manager, Instructional Technologist
- Industries: E-Learning, Professional Training & Coaching, Human Resources, Education Management, Hospital & Healthcare, Financial Services
- Company size: 200+ employees (programs at scale are the pain point)
- Geography: US, Canada, UK, Australia (English-language market for this webinar)
- Exclude: Students, entry-level job titles, companies under 50 employees

**Cold leadership audience (third priority — lower budget allocation):**
- Job titles: Chief Learning Officer, VP of Learning & Development, Director of Learning Operations, Director of Talent Development, VP of Talent
- Same industry and company size targeting as practitioner segment
- This segment converts at lower rates but higher average deal value downstream — useful for building the leader pipeline toward the alignment audit offer

### Content Type and Format

**Sponsored Content (Single Image Ad):** Lead format for retargeting segment. The pull quote visual from Devon's specs (InSync Blue background, white Montserrat Bold quote, Golden Honey accent line) is the primary creative. Use the Tuesday pull quote image.

**Sponsored Content (Document Ad / Carousel):** Wednesday's carousel adapted as a LinkedIn Document Ad for the cold practitioner audience. Document Ads show a preview of the carousel in feed with a download/view prompt — driving engagement before the webinar registration CTA. This format works well for the three-sign diagnostic format.

**Message Ad (InMail):** Consider for the retargeting segment only — not cold audiences. Message Ads to cold audiences produce low engagement and brand friction. For warm audiences (checklist downloaders specifically), a personalized InMail referencing "if you ran the checklist, the webinar is the next step" can drive high-quality registrations. Keep it under 150 words.

### Bid Strategy

Manual CPC with Enhanced CPC. This campaign does not have historical conversion data sufficient for Smart Bidding optimization (requires 50+ conversions in 30 days). Manual CPC gives budget control while the campaign builds conversion signal.

**Target CPC ranges:**
- Retargeting segment: $6–$10 CPC (warm audience, lower cost to click)
- Cold practitioner segment: $10–$18 CPC (LinkedIn B2B niche targeting is expensive; this range is realistic for the job title / industry combination)
- Cold leadership segment: $12–$20 CPC

**Target CPL (cost per webinar registration):**
- Retargeting: $30–$60 per registration
- Cold practitioner: $60–$120 per registration
- Blended target: $50–$90 per registration

At a $2,000 weekly budget, this translates to approximately 22–40 paid registrations per week, depending on audience mix and conversion rate.

### Reach and Impression Benchmarks

- Retargeting segment: 5,000–15,000 impressions/week depending on audience size
- Cold practitioner: 20,000–60,000 impressions/week
- CTR benchmark for this niche: 0.4–0.8% on single image; 0.6–1.2% on document/carousel

## 6. Landing Page Requirements

The webinar registration page must be confirmed live before any paid or organic links go out. This is a blocking dependency.

**Required elements for paid traffic to convert:**

- **Above the fold:** Webinar title, date/time, one-sentence value proposition ("Real case stories from facilitators, designers, and producers who made the alignment shift — plus a Designer Self-Assessment you leave with"), and registration form or prominent CTA button. Practitioners who click from a LinkedIn ad should be able to register within 15 seconds of landing.
- **Form length:** Maximum 4 fields for cold traffic: First Name, Last Name, Work Email, Job Title. Every additional field drops conversion rate 15–20%. If InSync's CRM requires more fields, add them as optional or post-registration.
- **Mobile optimization:** LinkedIn ad clicks are majority mobile. The registration form must be functional and completion-easy on mobile. Test before launch.
- **Trust signals:** InSync logo, brief mention of who will present (or "practitioners from [verticals] sharing real case stories"), and one proof data point (e.g., "Drawing on patterns from 6,000+ learning programs").
- **Page speed:** Target under 3 seconds load time. A slow registration page will kill conversion from paid traffic, which has a short attention window.
- **Conversion tracking:** Google Tag Manager event or LinkedIn Insight Tag conversion event must fire on form submission confirmation. Without conversion tracking, paid optimization is impossible and ROI cannot be measured.
- **Copy alignment:** The page headline must match ad headline language. If the ad says "Hear from the practitioners who made the shift," the page must use similar language. Mismatched ad-to-page copy tanks Quality Score and practitioner trust simultaneously.

## 7. Stat and Claim Credibility Assessment

This audience is sophisticated. A practitioner who challenges a statistic publicly — and is right — damages InSync's positioning in the community. Every stat requires a defensible source before it appears in paid creative.

| Claim | Source Status | Credibility Risk | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Time-to-competency improves 25%, rework falls 30% when alignment frameworks are enforced" | Attributed in brief to general research — specific source not cited | HIGH for a practitioner audience. These are precise numbers that invite challenge. If they cannot be traced to a named study, the precision is a liability. | For paid ads: replace with qualitative framing — "Teams that align design and delivery consistently report fewer revision cycles and faster learner application." Do not use the 25%/30% figures in ad copy without a citable source. Use in organic content at Jennifer Hofmann's attribution only. |
| "30% increase in employee knowledge retention with structured alignment tools" | In the brief without source citation | MEDIUM-HIGH. 30% retention improvement is a common round number in L&D — it may draw skepticism. | For paid ads: omit or attribute to a named source. If the source is Brandon Hall Group or similar, name them. |
| "4x more likely to report success — 6,000+ learning programs" | Strong — specific methodology claim | LOW if source is named. The "6,000+ programs" specificity lends credibility. | Use in paid copy with source attribution: "Data from 6,000+ learning programs (Brandon Hall Group)" or equivalent. Confirm the source before use. |
| "73% of organizations rely on vILT; only 27% consider it highly effective" | Strong — specific, commonly attributed to Brandon Hall Group | LOW if sourced. This stat is widely cited in L&D and will be recognized. | Use with source attribution in ad copy. Short format: "73% rely on vILT. Only 27% call it effective." |
| "Facilitators begin making fewer interpretation decisions in the moment" | Qualitative observation from Jennifer Hofmann's experience | LOW — this is a practitioner pattern claim, not a research claim, and is framed as such | Use as written in ad copy. Qualitative practitioner observations from a named expert are credible to this audience. |

**Blocking action before paid launch:** Confirm the primary sources for the 25%/30% and 30% knowledge retention figures. If they cannot be sourced to a named study, remove from ad copy and replace with qualitative framing. Do not run a challenged stat in paid media to a practitioner audience.
