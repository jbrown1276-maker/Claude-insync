## 1. Visual Concept Summary

This campaign looks like a before-and-after told through color temperature. Warm, slightly muted Ivory Mist carries the drift state — the familiar friction of a program running on interpretation and improvisation. Cool, clear Light Blue carries the aligned state — the specific, observable improvement that follows when the handoff works. These two colors do not fight each other. They exist in sequence, the way the argument exists in sequence. The core visual grammar is not decorative contrast — it is a proof structure rendered in color. Everything else in the system — the handoff motif from Week 2, the three-level sequence connector, the Golden Honey proof callouts — supports that central argument. The campaign looks disciplined and specific. It does not look aspirational. It looks like something a practitioner would trust.

**Golden Honey — #eda113**

In Week 2, Golden Honey signaled "new this week" — the checklist's color, the launch badge. That role is closed. Do not carry Week 2 Golden Honey treatments into Week 3 assets.
Usage: Level label tags in Blog 3 before-and-after visual. Golden Honey accent line in email header. Golden Honey left border on newsletter callout box. Accent line on LinkedIn Post 1 pull quote image. One instance per layout maximum.

**Color Hierarchy This Week:**
1. InSync Blue — structure and authority

## 3. Typography System

**Montserrat Bold (700)**
Function: All display headlines, carousel headers, featured image text, email header text, pull quote body text on dark backgrounds.
Size range: 28–48px for digital assets; 18–24px for carousel slides.
Campaign rule: The three-level sequence labels always appear in Montserrat Bold at the same size, weight, and spacing. No level gets more visual weight than another.

**Montserrat Medium (500)**
Function: Subheads, level labels in the before-and-after visual, newsletter callout box text, Golden Honey callout text.
Size range: 14–20px
Campaign rule: Golden Honey proof callout uses Montserrat Medium at 15px with 1.6 line height. Never Montserrat Bold in the callout box.

**Montserrat Regular (400)**
Function: Body copy in designed assets — email body text, carousel slide description lines, newsletter body.
Size range: 13–16px
Campaign rule: All one-sentence descriptions in before-and-after visual rows use Montserrat Regular. They must be visually quieter than the level label tags.

**Open Sans — fallback only**
Use when Montserrat fails to render. Extra Bold (headlines), Bold (subheads), Regular (body). Do not mix with Montserrat in the same asset.

**Hierarchy principle:** Three tiers — headline (Bold, large) → label/subhead (Medium, mid) → description (Regular, small). The three-level sequence always renders at the label tier — Medium weight, mid-size.

## 4. Core Visual Motif

**Carries forward from Week 2:** The handoff motif — line-weight (not filled) arrow between Design→Delivery, document with two sets of hands, checklist item being marked. Do not rebuild. Use what was built.

**New this week — The Three-Stage Converging Handoff:**
Structure: Three labeled nodes arranged along a converging line — extending the Week 2 drift-to-alignment line motif (two lines converging) into three sequential stages. Stages are chained, not parallel. Stage 2 is only reachable after Stage 1. Stage 3 only after Stage 2.

Construction: Line-weight strokes only, consistent with Week 2. Three nodes on converging line, evenly spaced. Each node: small unfilled circle (InSync Blue stroke, 2px weight) with label below in Montserrat Medium. Connector lines: InSync Blue, 1.5px stroke. Drift indicator at far left (two lines pulling apart from Week 2). Clean convergence point at far right.

States:
- Drift (before): open circles, dashed connector lines, Ivory Mist palette context
- Aligned (after): nodes fill Light Blue, solid connector lines, sequence reads complete

Scale behavior:
- Hero/featured image: full three-node system, ~400–500px wide within 1200px canvas
- Carousel slide: single node highlighted per slide, other two at reduced opacity
- Newsletter/callout: simplified three-dot sequence indicator

Must never look like: A horizontal org chart, a numbered steps illustration, a circular flow diagram, or a diverging fork/tree.

## 5. Photography and Imagery Direction

**Scenario:** The moment just before delivery begins. A producer at a second monitor reviewing a finalized run-of-show. A facilitator and instructional designer looking at the same screen in a pre-session briefing. A facilitator with a printed annotated delivery brief, focused, not anxious.

**Lighting:** Natural, cool-toned. No warm filters. No studio glow. Tuesday morning, not a hero moment.

**Subject behavior:** Focused, collaborative, calm. Not enthused. Not celebrating. Not pointing at screens with broad gestures.

**Color grading:** Slightly cool cast, slightly desaturated. Tonally coherent with the Ivory Mist / Light Blue palette.

**What to never shoot or use:**
- Smiling people in a conference room
- Anyone at a whiteboard with markers
- Hands pointing at a laptop in exaggerated gesture
- People looking at phones or tablets to represent "learning"
- Groups celebrating or applauding
- Solo executive looking out a window

**Stock sources:** Shutterstock, 123RF, Envato. Search terms: "virtual meeting preparation," "producer remote session," "instructional design review," "training delivery preparation," "facilitator document review."

**LinkedIn Post 3 specific:** Facilitator and producer at the same table or screen reviewing a run-of-show together, not looking at camera. The moment should feel observed, not staged.

## 7. Layout Principles

1. **Proof before ask, always.** No CTA appears above the fold in any asset. Evidence precedes button in every layout.
2. **The three-level sequence reads in one direction.** Facilitator Consistency → Producer Stability → Learner Readiness. No layout inverts this order for visual composition reasons.
3. **Color signals meaning before aesthetics.** Ivory Mist = before. Light Blue = after. Golden Honey = evidence. Cool Blue = action. Ask: does this color's meaning match what this element is communicating?
4. **One Golden Honey moment per layout.** Identify the single strongest evidence moment and mark it. Two Golden Honey elements compete and dilute both.
5. **Generous white space is structural in the carousel.** 40-word maximum per slide is a layout rule, not just a copy constraint. If a slide feels crowded, the copy is too long.
6. **The before/after visual is not a table.** No grid lines, no borders, no "Before/After" headers. The colors carry those meanings.
7. **Photography and color system coexist, not compete.** Cool-tone grading required. Warm-toned photography will fight the system. Grade before use.

---

## 8. What to Avoid

**Using Light Blue decoratively.** It carries the "after" signal. Every appearance in a structured layout means "something got better." Using it as a divider or flourish collapses the semantic system.

**Using Golden Honey for anything checklist-related.** Golden Honey was the checklist's color in Week 2. Any Week 2 Golden Honey treatment bleeding into Week 3 creates visual confusion. The role has changed — enforce it.

**The before/after visual as product comparison.** Product A vs. Product B, bullet features, "Winner" arrows, comparison table borders — these are Week 2's prohibited patterns. Week 3's layout is a proof structure. If it starts reading like a SaaS pricing page, collapse to alternating single-column rows.

**More than one Cool Blue element per asset outside CTAs.** If Cool Blue appears twice, the eye does not know which one to click.

**Carousel slides over 40 words.** If copy runs long, flag to Sarah rather than reducing font size.

**Leadership Series banners as primary visual elements.** Supporting material only — never hero.
