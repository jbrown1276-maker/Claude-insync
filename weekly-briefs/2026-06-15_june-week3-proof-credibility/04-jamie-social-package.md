## 1. Platform Strategy Summary

**Primary Platform: LinkedIn**

LinkedIn is the only platform in active play for Week 3 organic content. The InSync audience — L&D practitioners, instructional designers, producers, and L&D leadership — is concentrated on LinkedIn in professional browsing mode. This is where they engage with peer content, save resources, and make registration decisions. The webinar is a LinkedIn-native conversion event in terms of audience behavior.

**Algorithm considerations that affect execution:**

- **External links in the post body** carry a reach penalty on LinkedIn. Sarah's approved copy for Tuesday's post already places the webinar link in the body with intent — the post is short enough and the close strong enough that the peer-voice CTA earns it. This is a judgment call: the link in the body will slightly reduce organic reach but increases directness of the conversion ask. For Wednesday and Thursday, the webinar link stays in caption body (Wednesday) and body copy (Thursday) — both are long enough that practitioners who reach the CTA have self-selected.
- **Carousel format** consistently outperforms static images for saves and shares in the L&D niche. Wednesday's carousel is the highest-shareability asset this week and should be treated as the week's amplification priority.
- **Character limit:** LinkedIn post body is 3,000 characters. Thursday's post is the longest (~1,400 characters) — well within limit. No truncation risk on any post.
- **First comments** must be posted within 60 seconds of publish to surface alongside the post in feeds. This is non-negotiable. Have the first comment copy pre-loaded before hitting publish.
- **Document format for carousel:** LinkedIn supports native document/PDF carousels (which auto-play in feed and allow swipe) as well as image carousels. Recommend uploading as a PDF carousel (multi-page document) rather than individual images — native carousels get better feed real estate and auto-advance behavior encourages completion.

**Additional platforms — Week 3 recommendation:**

Do not extend to additional platforms this week. The practitioner and L&D leadership audience is not meaningfully reachable on Instagram, X, or TikTok at the volume that would justify creative adaptation time. If InSync has an email newsletter audience that overlaps with LinkedIn, the newsletter (handled by Sarah and Casey) is the correct second channel. Organic social stays on LinkedIn only.

## 4. Hashtag Strategy

**Overall approach:** Three tags per post, maximum. The L&D practitioner audience on LinkedIn is sophisticated enough to find over-hashtagged posts amateurish — three is the ceiling, and the tags earn their place.

**Selection logic:**
- One discovery anchor tag per post: #LearningAndDevelopment. High volume, used by the target audience, signals community membership.
- One argument-specific tag: varies by post. #VirtualLearning (Tuesday — facilitator argument), #DesignAndDelivery (Wednesday — the campaign's specific argument), #DesignAndDelivery again (Thursday — sustained argument).
- One audience-segment tag: #InstructionalDesign (Tuesday — designer-skewing argument), #LDProfessionals (Wednesday — practitioner community), #LDLeadership (Thursday — leadership-accessible close).

**What to avoid:** Do not use #Training, #eLearning, or #HR — these are high-volume but low-specificity and will surface the posts to an audience that does not recognize the practitioner-level argument. They generate impressions, not engagement from the right people.

## 5. Engagement Response Guide

**Comments engaging with the argument ("this is exactly what happens in our programs"):**
Respond within 2 hours during business hours. Acknowledge the specific thing they named. Ask one follow-up: "Which level — facilitator, producer, or learner — do you see it show up first?" This extends the conversation and surfaces practitioner-generated proof that reinforces the campaign argument. Do not redirect immediately to the webinar link.

**Questions about the webinar ("what exactly will be covered"):**
Respond with specifics: "The session is built on real case stories from facilitators, designers, and producers who've made this shift — plus a Designer Self-Assessment you leave with. Here's the registration link: [link]." One clear answer, one link, done.

**Skeptical comments ("we've heard alignment arguments before"):**
Do not defend the framework. Agree with the premise: "You're right that alignment talk is everywhere. That's exactly why Blog 3 focuses on what it actually produces rather than arguing for it. Worth a read: [blog link]." Turn the skepticism into a proof click.

**What to save/like during the campaign window:**
Any practitioner content that describes a facilitator or producer problem from their own experience — save it. Any post discussing vILT effectiveness challenges — like it. Any L&D leadership post about delivery consistency at scale — like and comment briefly. This behavior signals to the algorithm that InSync is an active member of these conversations, which improves organic reach on subsequent posts.

**Escalate to Jeff:**
Any press inquiry, any request to feature InSync in external content, any comment from a named potential client or competitor. Handle everything else independently.
