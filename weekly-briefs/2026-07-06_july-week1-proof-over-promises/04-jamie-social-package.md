## 5. ENGAGEMENT RESPONSE GUIDE

This section is for whoever monitors comments across the week. Read all of it before the first post goes live.

---

### General Tone Principles for All Responses

- Write as InSync Training (company voice), not as an individual. Short, direct, collegial.
- Never respond with exclamation points. Never use "Great point!" or "Love this question."
- Match the post's register: peer-to-peer, grounded, evidence-aware.
- Aim to respond to substantive comments within 2–4 hours during business hours, same day at minimum.
- Do not respond to every reaction (likes, celebrates) — only comments that open a conversation.

---

### Post 1 — Tuesday, July 7 (The question post)

**What to expect:**
- Direct answers to the closing question ("The one thing I wish I had was...") — these are the gold. They demonstrate the post landed and create social proof in the comment thread.
- Broad agreement ("This is so real" / "This is exactly where we are") — common, less substantive, but worth acknowledging briefly.
- Practitioner venting ("We've been saying this for years") — validate without escalating.
- Occasional pushback from measurement-tool vendors or L&D consultants arguing that the tools exist. This is expected and should not be engaged defensively.

**Priority engagement — respond to these first:**
- Anyone who answers the question with specifics. Ask a follow-up that goes one level deeper ("What made that particular data point feel like the right one?"). These responses signal to the algorithm that the post has active conversation and extend reach.
- Anyone who shares a specific situation (a meeting, a budget conversation, a program outcome). Acknowledge the specificity. Do not pivot to a product mention.

**Response tone model:**
> "That's a useful answer — the distinction between session engagement and post-session application is exactly the gap most dashboards skip over. It comes up a lot in how we think about measurement continuity."

**Do not:** Respond with a link to the White Paper. This post has no CTA — adding one in comments undermines the trust-building function of the post.

---

### Post 2 — Wednesday, July 8 (The stat post)

**What to expect:**
- Questions about the statistic source ("Where does this data come from?") — very common on stat posts.
- Agreement and amplification ("We see this everywhere" / "The 48% number is the one that surprises me every time").
- People sharing their own organizational measurement struggles.
- Skepticism about the stat ("Does this number seem high to anyone else?").

**Priority engagement — respond to these first:**
- Questions about the source. Respond immediately: "The data is from LEO Learning and Watershed — their 2023 research on L&D measurement practices. The original report is worth reading if you want the full context."
- Comments from people describing their own measurement gap. Acknowledge and bridge to the White Paper naturally: "If you're working through this, the Why Go Live White Paper addresses the structural question directly — it's linked in the first comment."

**Response tone model:**
> "The 48% is the number that tends to land hardest. Completion is easy to capture — the infrastructure for it was built into every LMS. What's harder is building evaluation practices that capture what happens in the 60 days after the session ends."

**Do not:** Repeat the stat in your response — the person you're responding to just read it.

---

### Post 3 — Thursday, July 9 (The Hofmann / White Paper post)

**What to expect:**
- People asking about the Hofmann article or where to find it (they missed the first comment) — direct them to the first comment.
- Practitioners who have worked with Jennifer Hofmann or InSync sharing positive experiences — engage warmly, keep it brief.
- CLOs and VPs sharing the post or commenting substantively on the measurement framing — highest-value comments of the week.
- Questions about the White Paper content ("What does the case study from Cisco actually show?").

**Priority engagement — respond to these first:**
- CLO/VP-level commenters. Respond thoughtfully, acknowledge their context specifically, do not redirect to a sales conversation.
- Anyone asking about the White Paper's specifics. Give a direct, substantive answer: "The Cisco case study focuses on how they redesigned their facilitation model to include structured behavior checkpoints — the measurement story got easier once the design intent was explicit."
- Comments tagging colleagues ("Sending this to our CLO") — reply with a simple: "Worth the conversation."

**If Jennifer Hofmann engages or amplifies from her personal account:**
This is a signal moment. Respond briefly and collegially from the InSync page: "Thank you, Jennifer — the framing in your piece is the clearest articulation of the structural problem we've seen." Do not turn it into a promotional moment.

**Response tone model:**
> "Jennifer's piece is the one we'd recommend reading with your CLO before the next budget cycle. The framework she introduces changes what question you're asking — which changes what data you need."
