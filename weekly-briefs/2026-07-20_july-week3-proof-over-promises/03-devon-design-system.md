## 1. Visual Concept Summary

Week 3 is the visual payoff of three weeks of structured incompleteness. The gap motif has been open and then narrowing — this week it reads as actively resolving, three-quarters closed with Cool Blue, legible to anyone who has followed the arc and interpretable to anyone seeing it for the first time. The campaign concept "The System Is Knowable" is expressed visually not through diagrams or infographics but through authority: dark fields, clean typography, a document that looks like something a CLO would pick up. The White Paper is the visual hero this week, not a photograph, not a person. Where Weeks 1 and 2 held tension in the gap, Week 3 releases it into proof. The motif does not close — that is Week 4's job — but it signals unmistakably that something has been resolved. Cool Blue replaces Golden Honey (fully retired) as the dominant accent color throughout every asset. Every design decision this week should answer Elena's north star question: does this look like something a CLO would take seriously?

## 2. Color System

### InSync Blue `#032b43`
**Role:** Primary field. Background authority across all assets.
**Usage:** Full-bleed background for all email headers, newsletter header block, all LinkedIn cards, White Paper preview dark field, motif field.
**Rules:** Never use as text color. Never used for accents or dividers — that is Medium Blue's function. The dark field is non-negotiable and carries directly from Weeks 1 and 2.
**Must never:** Appear as a light-surface treatment or be lightened for any purpose.

### Cool Blue `#0883cf`
**Role:** Predominant signal color. This is the Week 3 color.
**Usage:** CTA button backgrounds ("Download the White Paper"), gap-fill in the motif graphic at 70–75% fill state, highlight rules and underline accents, data callout backgrounds in newsletter, the diagonal band / structural accent on LinkedIn Wednesday card. Must appear in every asset — it is the visual proof that something is resolving.
**Rules:** Use with intention; it should read as signal, not decoration. CTA buttons at full-fill Cool Blue, white text. Motif fill exactly 70–75% — do not overfill.
**Must never:** Be used as a warm or neutral background surface. Must not be mixed with Golden Honey in any form.

### Medium Blue `#044269`
**Role:** Secondary container. Depth layer.
**Usage:** Section dividers in newsletter and email, secondary card backgrounds where a subordinate content panel is needed, sidebar panel backgrounds. Keeps layout depth without competing with Cool Blue's signal function.
**Rules:** Never used as the primary field — that belongs to InSync Blue. Never for headlines or CTAs.
**Must never:** Be the dominant color in any asset. It recedes.

### Light Blue `#00b9f5`
**Role:** Energy accent. Single use per asset.
**Usage this week:** Reserved for the selected quote text on LinkedIn Thursday card only — "Strong facilitators are the product of a strong system." (Devon's selection; see LinkedIn Thursday spec below.) If Light Blue is used on Thursday, it does not appear elsewhere that week. If a newsletter or email layout needs a secondary accent, omit Light Blue rather than use it twice in the same send.
**Rules:** One appearance per asset maximum. If every asset uses it, it loses its function entirely.
**Must never:** Appear in more than one location within a single asset. Never used for body copy or secondary text.

### Slate Grey `#6e8699`
**Role:** Body text, captions, secondary copy.
**Usage:** All body copy in email and newsletter. Caption text under images or preview graphics. Secondary copy blocks that follow a headline. Used in production handoff labels if needed.
**Rules:** Not for headlines. Not for CTA text. Not for motif elements.
**Must never:** Be used where Cool Blue belongs — Slate Grey is for reading, Cool Blue is for acting.

### Ivory Mist `#d9d4c5`
**Role:** Warm offset. Section breathing room in newsletter only.
**Usage:** Newsletter section backgrounds where a warm separation from the InSync Blue field is needed — the body-copy section of the newsletter between the header and the CTA block. Use it sparingly; it is a warmth note and a contrast surface, not a primary field.
**Must never:** Appear in LinkedIn cards, email header, or the motif. It belongs to the newsletter only, and only in one section per issue.

### Golden Honey `#eda113`
**FULLY RETIRED FOR WEEK 3.** Does not appear in any asset in any capacity — not as a divider, not as a micro-accent, not as a hover state, not anywhere. If it appears in any Week 3 file, remove it before delivery. No exceptions.

### Usage Hierarchy
1. InSync Blue — all dark fields
2. Cool Blue — all signal moments (CTAs, motif fill, key accents)
3. Medium Blue — secondary containers and dividers
4. Slate Grey — body and secondary copy
5. Light Blue — one accent moment in one asset (LinkedIn Thursday)
6. Ivory Mist — newsletter body section background only

## 3. Typography System

### Montserrat Bold
**Usage:** All headlines across every asset. Newsletter header headline, email header headline, all LinkedIn card headlines, White Paper preview title text.
**Size range:** 28–48px for card and email headlines. Newsletter header headline: 36–42px. LinkedIn card headlines: 28–34px depending on character count.
**Weight:** Bold (700).
**Case rule:** Sentence case only. No all-caps headlines, no title case for cards. "Scale is not repetition." — not "SCALE IS NOT REPETITION." The weight carries the authority; caps add the wrong energy.
**Must never:** Be used for body copy or CTAs (Medium is correct there). Must never be set all-caps.

### Montserrat Medium
**Usage:** Subheadings within newsletter and email body, CTA button text, any White Paper document preview subheader.
**Size range:** 14–18px for subheads, 16–18px for CTA buttons.
**Weight:** Medium (500).
**Case rule:** Sentence case for subheads. CTA button text: "Download the White Paper" — sentence case, not title case.
**Must never:** Be used for headline-level copy — that belongs to Bold.

### Montserrat Regular
**Usage:** All body copy in email and newsletter. Footer text and legal lines if present.
**Size range:** 14–16px.
**Weight:** Regular (400).
**Must never:** Be used for headlines, subheads, or CTAs.

### Montserrat Light
**Usage:** Pull quotes and callout copy set at larger display size. If a newsletter or email uses a pullout quote or a large supporting statement, set it in Light. The contrast between Light weight at display size and surrounding Bold headlines is the typographic signature of the Q3 series.
**Size range:** 20–26px when used as a display pull quote.
**Weight:** Light (300).
**Must never:** Be used for small-size text — it disappears. Light is for large-format callout moments only.

### Hierarchy principle
One size jump between levels. The layout must be readable at a glance: headline (Bold, largest) → subhead (Medium, one jump down) → body (Regular, one jump below subhead) → CTA (Medium, same level as subhead but differentiated by button treatment). Never skip levels. Never use the same size for two different hierarchy roles.

## 4. Core Visual Motif — The Gap (Week 3 State)

### What it represents
The gap motif is the Q3 campaign's structural metaphor: the distance between the measurement tools L&D leaders have and the ones they need. Week 1 opened the gap. Week 2 narrowed it slightly. Week 3 is the near-resolution: the gap is filling with Cool Blue, representing proof entering the space where uncertainty lived.

### Construction
Two parallel horizontal lines — thin, approximately 2–3px — running the full width of the asset's horizontal field. Set against InSync Blue `#032b43` background. Between the two lines, a fill band: the left 70–75% of the horizontal span filled solid with Cool Blue `#0883cf`. The right 25–30% remains open — InSync Blue background shows through the gap. The fill reads left to right, as something actively completing from the origin point.

**Proportions for each format:**
- **Full-width horizontal (newsletter header, email header):** Lines span 100% of asset width. Fill begins at the left margin and extends 70–75% across. The open gap sits right-aligned. Minimum total motif band height: 8px. Recommended: 12–16px for newsletter, 6–8px for email (below logo).
- **Strip (LinkedIn card):** Same left-to-right fill logic, sized to the card width. Motif strip height: 6–8px. Sits horizontally below the headline, full card width.
- **Corner element (White Paper preview):** Scaled down to a small horizontal element — approximately 80–100px wide — positioned in the lower right corner of the document preview graphic, below the document title area. Proportions preserved (70–75% fill). Subordinate; the document is the hero.

### Fill specifics
- Fill color: Cool Blue `#0883cf` only. No gradients. Flat fill.
- Fill percentage: 70–75%. If measuring at 600px wide, the filled portion is 420–450px. Do not eyeball this; set it precisely.
- The fill reads as active/resolving, not decorative. It should feel like progress, not ornamentation.

### What the motif must NOT do this week
- Return to the full-open state from Week 1 (0% fill or minimal fill).
- Close completely — 100% fill is Week 4 territory only.
- Use Golden Honey for any portion of the fill or surrounding treatment.
- Use any color other than Cool Blue `#0883cf` for the fill element.
- Become three-dimensional, shadowed, or gradient-filled — flat construction only.

## 5. Photography and Imagery Direction

### Week 3 scenario — The desk with the document
Subject: a leader reviewing a substantial document. Not a report on a screen, not a meeting — a physical document being read with intent. The activity is purposeful: the subject has found what they needed and is now acting on it.

**Subject behavior:** Quiet focus. Not stressed, not excited. The expression is the look of someone who has found the right frame for an argument they have been trying to make. Document in hands or open on the desk. Posture: seated or leaning in slightly — engaged, not passive.

**Environment:** Clean desk. Minimal props — one pen, one glass of water maximum. One window in the background providing natural light with a cool tone. No clutter. No second monitor. No coffee cups with visible logos. Authority through reduction.

**Lighting:** Natural light, cool tone. Consistent with the Weeks 1 and 2 lighting register. No warm filters. No golden-hour color cast. If color grading is applied, pull the temperature slightly cool to match the Cool Blue palette.

**Color grading direction:** Desaturate slightly, pull toward cool mid-tones. The image should feel contemporarily documentary, not stylized or aspirational.

**What to never use:**
- Groups of people. This is an individual professional moment.
- Screens. The White Paper is a document — the imagery reflects that materiality.
- Generic business meeting stock — conference tables, whiteboards, people pointing at slides.
- Stress or frustration expressions. Week 3 is resolution territory. Not anxiety, not struggle.
- Warm filter stock that introduces golden tones — it invokes Golden Honey, which is retired.

### Photography applies to: newsletter (header optional), email (none this week — see channel specs)
Newsletter and email this week de-emphasize photography. The White Paper document preview is the visual hero. Photography, if used at all, is secondary to the document visual — it does not appear on LinkedIn cards.

## 7. Layout Principles

1. **Vertical hierarchy, not horizontal grids.** Every asset uses a single-column vertical structure. Content flows top to bottom: logo, motif element, headline, supporting copy or visual, CTA. Do not introduce multi-column grids in email or newsletter.

2. **The White Paper has its own visual presence.** Wherever the CTA appears — in email, newsletter, or LinkedIn — the White Paper is represented as a document, not as a text link or generic graphic. The document preview asset must accompany every primary CTA placement.

3. **One Cool Blue signal per visual region.** In any given vertical section of a layout, Cool Blue should appear in one intentional role — motif fill, CTA button background, structural accent. If Cool Blue appears in two adjacent elements, one of them is wrong.

4. **Generous whitespace.** Week 3 is authoritative and calm. Padding between sections: minimum 32px. Padding within sections: minimum 20px. The layout should breathe. Do not fill empty space with additional content.

5. **Motif placement is load-bearing, not decorative.** The gap motif is not a divider or a decorative line element. It is the campaign's structural signature. Place it where it marks a transition — below the logo in email (before the headline), below the headline in LinkedIn cards (between the statement and the field), at the base of the document preview (at the boundary between the document and the CTA zone).

6. **Type contrast is visual structure.** Montserrat Bold white headlines on InSync Blue do not need additional graphic framing. Do not add background boxes, shadows, or rules around headline text. The contrast of white on dark does the structural work.

7. **The LinkedIn card is complete as designed.** Do not add secondary copy, smaller-type details, or additional visual elements to LinkedIn cards beyond what is specified. Minimalism is the message on Thursday in particular; Tuesday and Wednesday also hold to this. Every addition to a card makes it a poster. That is the wrong register.

## 8. What to Avoid

**Infographic-style explainer layouts for the Live Learning Formula.** If the Live Learning Formula appears in any graphic context, it is named as text — three plain items in a horizontal or vertical list, set in Montserrat. Not a diagram with icons, circular nodes, or arrows. The system is expressed through language precision, not visual taxonomy.

**Motivational poster energy.** Large display-size quotes set over a stock photography background communicate the wrong register. The Thursday LinkedIn card uses a quote at significant size, but it is on a clean dark field — the opposite of motivational poster treatment. White text on a relevant photo is never correct this week.

**Over-produced White Paper preview.** The document preview should look like a document. No starburst, no "FREE DOWNLOAD" badge, no decorative border that reads as a marketing asset. Clean, formal, executive-weight. The more it looks like a designed marketing piece, the less authority it carries.

**Golden Honey in any context.** Not as a micro-accent on a divider. Not as a hover state. Not in a pre-existing template element that was not updated. Review every file before delivery and confirm the hex value `#eda113` does not appear.

**Full gap closure.** The motif fill reaches 75% maximum this week. If any asset shows the gap fully closed, it must be corrected — that visual belongs to Week 4 and cannot appear this week without disrupting the arc.

**Decorative Cool Blue use.** Cool Blue must read as signal — it marks something the audience should act on (CTA), or it marks the campaign's structural progress (motif fill), or it provides a structural divider (Wednesday LinkedIn vertical accent). Cool Blue as a decorative stripe, border, or texture element is not correct. Every Cool Blue application must have a function.

**Generic stock photography.** If photography is sourced for the newsletter, it must match the Week 3 scenario: individual, document, clean desk, cool light. A group of people at a conference table is not compliant. An empty conference room with a phone in the center is not compliant. The scenario is specific; hold to it.
