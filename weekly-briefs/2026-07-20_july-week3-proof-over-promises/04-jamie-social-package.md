## 1. Platform Strategy Summary

### Platform in Play: LinkedIn (Organic + Potential Paid Boost)

**Why LinkedIn only this week:**
Week 3's emotional territory is proof. The audience — CLOs, VP L&Ds, Learning Directors — evaluates claims against evidence. LinkedIn is the platform where that audience is professionally active, sharing content laterally with peers and directly with executives. It is the only platform where a 241-word evidence-based argument earns impressions rather than fighting the algorithm. Twitter/X and Meta channels carry a different register; they are not suited to the systems-lens argument Jennifer Hofmann's blog makes or to the credibility stakes of a White Paper feature post.

**LinkedIn algorithm considerations this week:**
- Document/image posts with 1200×628 cards consistently outperform link-only posts in niche B2B verticals. All three posts use Devon's cards — correct format.
- Posts with high early engagement (first 60–90 minutes) receive disproportionate distribution. First-comment timing is a distribution lever, not a courtesy — post within 60 seconds of publish without exception.
- Three posts across three consecutive days creates impression frequency without triggering over-posting penalties (LinkedIn's informal 1 post/day guideline is per-account, not per-week).
- Tuesday is the strongest organic reach day in B2B L&D on LinkedIn. Thursday benefits from week-end decision-making cadence. Wednesday is lower reach but highest intent for download CTAs.

**Week 3 posting logic:**
- Tuesday: Education post. Build the conceptual case. Earn organic reach through shareability. Practitioner and leader both recognize themselves.
- Wednesday: White Paper feature. Anchor the week's conversion moment. Fewer impressions but higher intent audience reading mid-week.
- Thursday: Social proof / amplification. Close the week on evidence. Both audience segments addressed. Reinforces the download decision for anyone who hesitated Wednesday.

## 5. Engagement Response Guide

### Comments engaging with the argument
These are the most valuable interactions. Treat them as a continuation of the thought, not as a win to celebrate.

**Examples:** "This is the challenge we're facing right now," "We've been trying to solve exactly this," "Which of the three formula components breaks down most often in your experience?"

**Response approach:**
- Respond within 2–4 hours during business hours (8 AM–6 PM ET)
- Engage with the specific point the commenter made, not the general topic
- If they name their industry or context, acknowledge it specifically: "In manufacturing specifically, the Support and Learning Continuity piece tends to be the pressure point — geographically distributed teams, facilitators flying in."
- Do not push the White Paper download at someone who is already in a conversation. Let the conversation breathe. If they ask where to learn more, then route to the White Paper or the consultation.
- One follow-up question is appropriate; two is a conversation; three looks like an interview

**Escalate to Jeff if:** The commenter identifies themselves as a CLO or VP at a named organization and is asking substantive questions about their program. This is a sales conversation. Jeff owns it.

### Skeptical comments
**Examples:** "We tried live learning at scale and it didn't hold," "Facilitators matter more than systems in my experience," "This sounds good in theory but hard to implement."

**Response approach:**
- Acknowledge the experience directly. Do not argue. "That experience is common — and it's usually a signal that one of the three formula components was under-resourced, not that the live learning model was wrong."
- If their skepticism is specific (e.g., "We had inconsistent facilitators"), respond to that specific layer: "Design and Development sets the standard before the facilitator enters the room. If that component is weak, even strong facilitators can't compensate consistently."
- Do not discount the skepticism. The audience respects people who engage with hard cases.
- Avoid defensive language: "actually," "to be fair," "I would argue"

**Escalate to Jeff if:** The skeptic names a competitor or makes a claim about a specific negative experience with InSync.

### Questions
**Examples:** "Where can I find the White Paper?", "What does the InQuire Framework measure exactly?", "How do I get started with a consultation?"

**Response approach:**
- Direct questions about the White Paper: reply with the URL in the comment. Do not just say "Link in first comment" — they're already past the first comment if they're asking.
- Questions about InQuire: "The InQuire Engagement Framework measures the quality of three engagement conditions — environmental, intellectual, and emotional — that predict whether learning will transfer to performance. It's not a form; it's an observation lens facilitators and producers use during delivery." Then offer the White Paper or consultation for more.
- Questions about consultation: route directly to `https://info.insynctraining.com/insync-training`

**Escalate to Jeff if:** Anyone asks about pricing, implementation scope, enterprise contracts, or pilot programs.
