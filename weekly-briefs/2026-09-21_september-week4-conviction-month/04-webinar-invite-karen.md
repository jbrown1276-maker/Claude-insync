## Body

Hi [First name],

In 25 years of building and evaluating live learning, I have watched the same thing happen in nearly every organization I work with. The evaluation data comes in, someone files the satisfaction scores, the completion rate goes on a slide, and the program runs again unchanged for the next cohort.

That is not a data problem. It is a design problem, and it is fixable.

On September 24, our team is running a working session on exactly this: how to use the evaluation data you already collect to redesign live learning and move the outcomes your leadership cares about. It is called "How to Use Evaluation Data to Drive Live Learning Redesign," and it walks through five shifts that separate the organizations that improve their programs from the ones that only measure them.

I asked our team to make it practical, not theoretical. You will leave with:

- Using Evaluation to Improve Live Learning, a companion guide that shows how the five shifts apply across virtual, hybrid, and in-person design
- The 5 Shifts framework itself, a clear model for rethinking evaluation across design, delivery, and follow-up
- Templates your designers, facilitators, and evaluation leaders can put to work on the next program

If improving live learning is on your desk this quarter, this is the session I would want you in.
