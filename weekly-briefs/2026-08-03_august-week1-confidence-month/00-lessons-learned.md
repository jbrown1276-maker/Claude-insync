## What Worked

**Marcus's diagnostic frame held up.**
The "challenge without judgment" framing — asking "have you looked honestly?" rather than "you are failing" — gave Elena and Sarah a clear authority register to work from. Every channel stayed in that lane without policing.

**Cisco case used as benchmark, not sales pitch.**
Sixteen years / 24,000 employees / 92% exceeding expectations appeared in Thursday LinkedIn and Email #1 only. Correct placement. The stat arrived after the reader felt the problem. Did not appear in Newsletter (cut correctly by Sarah per Elena's note — the stat carries more weight in dedicated placement than diluted across all channels).

**Two-channel strategy for Stage 3 (Devon + Sarah) worked cleanly.**
Running both in parallel, then presenting outputs together, gave Jeff a unified approval gate rather than two separate reviews. No conflict between Devon's spec and Sarah's copy direction.

**Stat correction caught before approval.**
"Four thousand participants" corrected to "four thousand three hundred participants" pre-approval. Stat verification rule working.

**Elena final polish as pre-approval gate.**
Having Elena do a direct edit pass before presenting to Jeff reduced the number of revision loops. The only issue (spelling) was a new error introduced by the polish pass, not a brief compliance gap. Process itself is sound.

## What Needed Revision

**Sarah v1 violated word counts across all five channels.**
Newsletter 197 (limit 150), Tuesday 131 (limit 100), Wednesday 147 (limit 120), Thursday 145 (limit 120), Email #1 249 (limit 200). Elena flagged all five. This was the primary revision trigger — not voice or strategy misalignment.

**Root cause:** Sarah's first-pass instinct is to write full and then cut. The word limits need to be stated explicitly in the invocation brief, not just inherited from the channel direction. Brief Marcus to flag limits in her input.

**Elena introduced a spelling error during the polish pass.**
Elena changed "Lindsay-Finan" to "Lindsey-Finan." Caught by cross-referencing `00-blog1-source.md`. Correct spelling: **Jennifer Lindsay-Finan** (with 'a'). Always verify author names against source file before approving any agent's edits to proper nouns.

## What to Brief Differently Next Week

1. **State word limits in Sarah's invocation brief explicitly.** Do not assume she carries them from channel direction. List: Newsletter ≤150, LinkedIn ≤120 (Tue/Wed/Thu), Email #1 ≤200 body. Copy these limits verbatim into her prompt.

2. **Verify all proper nouns before presenting any agent output.** Especially author names, company names, and titles. Cross-check against source file, not brief.

3. **Stat placeholder protocol for Week 2.** The brief flags TalentLMS 2026 as the replacement for the Deloitte stat, but that stat has not been verified. Marcus should hold the stat space and note it explicitly rather than inventing a placeholder. Sarah needs to see a confirmed stat or write around it.

4. **Devon SVG production is a separate invocation.** Design system approval ≠ files built. Week 1 SVG assets not yet produced — this is an open production item, not a lessons-learned failure. But it must not be forgotten when production resumes.
