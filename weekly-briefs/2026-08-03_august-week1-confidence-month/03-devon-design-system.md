## 1. Visual Concept Summary

This campaign looks like a diagnostic instrument, not a marketing campaign. Every asset is built on InSync Blue — deep, clinical, authoritative — with Cool Blue introduced as a functional signal: the line that is beginning to resolve. There is no warmth, no celebration, no invitation. The visual language is borrowed from measurement infrastructure: session observation dashboards, data readouts, framework containers, behavioral signal cards. The reader sees a system that measures things. The implicit question is whether their system does the same. Typography is spare and dominant — one headline per asset, no decorative elements around it. White space is load-bearing. The Cisco numbers are not designed around; they are the design. Every asset should feel like it was produced by the same quality infrastructure InSync is describing.

## 2. Color System

### Primary Background
**InSync Blue · #032b43**  
Role: The dominant surface for all assets this week. Blog hero, Leadership Article hero, email header, newsletter header, LinkedIn posts.  
Usage rule: Full-bleed background only. Never used as text color. Never lightened or modified with opacity.  
Must never: appear at less than 100% opacity, be combined with gradients as decoration, or be replaced by any other dark color.

### Secondary Container
**Medium Blue · #044269**  
Role: Card backgrounds, section breaks, interior container fills where visual separation is needed without breaking the color register.  
Usage rule: Use when a surface sits on top of InSync Blue and needs distinction. Not a background for full assets.  
Must never: be used as a primary background, or combined with Ocean Blue at equal hierarchy (they are too close in value).

### Arc Motif Signal
**Cool Blue · #0883cf**  
Role: The color of measurement becoming visible. Reserved for CTAs, the arc motif line element, and any bar or line representing a measurement that is now resolvable.  
Usage rule: Use in exactly one place per asset. Its presence signals: this is what a measuring system looks like. On LinkedIn Tuesday, it appears as a thin horizontal rule at the bottom — partially resolved, not full-width. On all other assets, it appears as the CTA button fill or the structural line element only.  
Must never: be used as a container color (Ocean Blue owns that role), as a number highlight on stat cards (numbers are white), or used more than once in a single asset.

### Energy Accent
**Light Blue · #00b9f5**  
Role: Badge elements and number highlights only. Extremely limited use.  
Usage rule: Maximum one instance per asset. Only on badge-type callouts or a single number in a stat display that needs differentiation from surrounding white type.  
Must never: be used for headlines, body copy, or any full-surface fill. If in doubt, do not use it — Cool Blue is the safer default.

### Supporting Text
**Slate Grey · #6e8699**  
Role: Captions, attribution lines, source labels on stat cards, secondary copy in layouts.  
Usage rule: All text that is subordinate to the headline. Stat card source attribution below the number. "InQuire Engagement Framework" footer label on LinkedIn Wednesday.  
Must never: be used at headline weight or hierarchy, or be combined with Medium Blue as a background (insufficient contrast).

### Warm Neutral
**Ivory Mist · #d9d4c5**  
Role: Data offset sections only — a surface that reads as "result" or "evidence isolated from the system." Not a primary color this week.  
Usage rule: Use only if an asset requires a data panel or call-out box that needs visual separation from the primary InSync Blue field. Zero use in headlines or structural elements.  
Must never: be used as a dominant surface, appear on LinkedIn posts (wrong temperature for this register), or be confused with white.

### IQF Container Color
**Ocean Blue · #0f698c**  
Role: LinkedIn Wednesday IQF three-container backgrounds only. Distinguishes the three containers as a system from the CTA and arc-motif use of Cool Blue.  
Usage rule: Solid fill for all three IQF containers. Text inside is white. No opacity variations.  
Must never: be used outside the IQF LinkedIn Wednesday asset.

### IQF Connector
**Aqua Sky · #3ba7bf**  
Role: IQF dimension labels or connector lines on LinkedIn Wednesday only.  
Usage rule: Use for label text or thin connector lines between or above the three containers. Maximum use: two instances per asset.  
Must never: be used in any asset other than LinkedIn Wednesday.

### RETIRED — ABSOLUTE PROHIBITION
**Golden Honey · #eda113**  
Not as highlight, accent, data viz element, border, icon, or decoration. Not in any asset, in any form, at any opacity. This color does not exist in August.

### Usage Hierarchy
1. InSync Blue — dominant surface (100% of all backgrounds)
2. White — primary type
3. Cool Blue — single signal element per asset
4. Ocean Blue — IQF containers only (LinkedIn Wednesday)
5. Medium Blue — secondary container fills only
6. Slate Grey — subordinate text
7. Aqua Sky — IQF connectors only (LinkedIn Wednesday)
8. Light Blue — badge or single-number accent only, zero or one instance
9. Ivory Mist — data offset panel only, use sparingly

## 3. Typography System

### Primary Headline
**Montserrat Bold**  
Usage: All primary headlines, all assets, no exceptions.  
Size range: 36–64px depending on canvas size. Blog and Leadership Article hero: 48–60px. LinkedIn posts: 42–52px. Newsletter and email headers: 32–40px.  
Weight: Bold (700).  
Color: White (#ffffff) on all dark backgrounds.  
Campaign rule: One headline per asset. Sentence case throughout. No all-caps. No decorative treatment around the headline — the type is the element.

### Subheads and Stat Labels
**Montserrat Medium**  
Usage: Subheads, stat callout labels above numbers, dimension names inside IQF containers, the explanatory line below the Cisco stat cluster.  
Size range: 16–24px.  
Weight: Medium (500).  
Color: White for primary context; Slate Grey for attribution and secondary descriptors.  
Campaign rule: Title case for stat labels only (e.g., "Years of Partnership," "Employees Trained"). Sentence case everywhere else.

### Body and Caption
**Montserrat Regular**  
Usage: Body copy in multi-text layouts, source attribution below stat numbers, caption text, "InQuire Engagement Framework" footer label on LinkedIn Wednesday, brief descriptors on stat cards.  
Size range: 12–18px.  
Weight: Regular (400).  
Color: Slate Grey for captions and attribution. White for body copy inside containers.

### Email/Newsletter Fallback
**Open Sans Regular**  
Usage: Body copy fallback in email and newsletter environments where Montserrat may not render.  
Size: Match Montserrat Regular sizing (14–16px for email body).  
Weight: Regular (400).  
Color: Same as Montserrat Regular equivalent.  
Rule: Do not substitute Open Sans for Montserrat in headlines. Headlines use Montserrat Bold with web-safe fallback stack: `'Montserrat', 'Open Sans', Arial, sans-serif`.

### Hierarchy Principle
One typeface family. Three weights only — Bold for command, Medium for label, Regular for support. The size contrast between headline (Bold) and everything else must be significant — minimum 2:1 ratio. If the headline doesn't dominate, the layout is wrong.

## 4. Core Visual Motif

**The Incomplete Line That Is Resolving**

What it represents: A measurement system that was not tracking is now beginning to track. The line is not complete — completion is Week 4 of August. Week 1, the line is measurably more resolved than July, but still open.

Construction: A single horizontal rule. Full canvas width available to the line. In July Week 1, the line had a gap — an open break marked with Golden Honey. In August Week 1, that gap is smaller. The line is approximately 75–80% resolved: it runs from left edge, pauses at roughly 78% of the width, and the remaining 20–22% is absent. The line color is Cool Blue (#0883cf). Stroke weight: 2px on social assets, 1.5px on email and newsletter headers.

States this week:
- Blog hero: The motif line appears as a structural element integrated into the layout — not floating decoration. Position: bottom 15% of the canvas, full-width container, partially resolved as described.
- Newsletter header: The motif line is the primary structural element. It sits below the headline, same construction.
- LinkedIn Tuesday: One thin Cool Blue line at the bottom of the asset, partially resolved. It does not have a label. It reads as evidence, not decoration.
- LinkedIn Wednesday and Thursday: The motif line does not appear — these assets have their own structural logic (IQF containers, stat card). Introducing the motif line would compete.
- Leadership Article hero and email header: The motif line is optional — use only if the layout has sufficient whitespace to carry it without crowding. If in doubt, omit.

What it must never look like:
- A loading bar or progress indicator (it is not interactive)
- A decorative underline beneath a headline
- A divider rule between sections (it is not a divider — it is a motif)
- A full-width resolved line (resolution is Week 4)
- Any color other than Cool Blue

Scaling rule: On smaller canvases (newsletter, email header at 600px), maintain the same 78% resolved proportion. Do not simplify to a full line because the canvas is smaller.

## 5. Photography and Imagery Direction

**Preferred scenarios:**
- Virtual classroom interface with active chat rail and visible participant count — screen-captured or illustrated as a data readout, not photographed as "people on a video call"
- Session observation framework with some fields completed and some empty — the incompleteness is intentional
- Dashboard displaying session quality metrics where several dimensions show data and one or two remain at zero or blank
- Data debrief view — a facilitator's post-session readout, numbers and categories, no people visible

**Lighting:** Cool, blue-toned. Consistent with the InSync Blue register. No warm tones. No golden-hour color temperature. If an abstract composition uses light effects, those effects trend toward blue-white, not amber-white.

**If subjects appear:** They are in a specific act of observation or documentation. Looking at a screen with data. Writing into a framework. Not presenting. Not celebrating. Not smiling at camera. Subjects are not the subject — the system they are observing is.

**Abstract alternatives (strongly preferred):** Data visualization in progress. Systems diagrams with components in varying states of completion. Session quality dashboards. These are preferred over photography because they are more controllable, more consistent with the measurement-UI aesthetic, and do not risk the wrong emotional register.

**Never use:**
- Group photos of engaged learners
- Stock "aha moment" facial expressions
- Inspiration-poster aesthetics (sunlight, coffee, notebooks)
- Photorealistic open-plan office environments
- Hands on whiteboards
- Any image that could appear in a "learning is fun" campaign

**For LinkedIn posts:** No photography. These are typographic and data-visual assets.

## 7. Layout Principles

1. **White space is structural, not a gap to fill.** Every asset has visible InSync Blue that is doing nothing. That is not a problem — it is a feature. The diagnostic register requires room to breathe. Fill impulses are wrong.

2. **One headline, one job.** Each asset carries exactly one Montserrat Bold headline. That headline and the key visual tell the same story. If the headline asks a question, the key visual represents what a yes-answer looks like. They do not repeat each other — they complete each other.

3. **The number is the creative on stat assets.** On LinkedIn Thursday (Cisco), the design's job is to deliver three numbers at maximum legibility. Nothing else competes. No chart, no bar, no frame.

4. **One axis of alignment per asset.** LinkedIn posts are center-aligned. Email headers and newsletter headers are left-aligned. Do not mix axes within a single asset. Headlines, numbers, and labels align to one axis and hold it.

5. **Maximum two brand accent colors per asset, plus white and one neutral.** InSync Blue (background) + white (primary type) + one accent (Cool Blue OR Ocean Blue — never both in the same asset) + Slate Grey (captions). Light Blue and Ivory Mist are additions only when specifically justified by the asset logic. Count before you finish. If you are at three accent colors, remove one.

6. **Cool Blue appears once per asset, in one place.** It is a signal. Repetition dilutes the signal. Choose where it appears and hold that decision.

7. **Logo placement is fixed by format.** Bottom-right on all LinkedIn posts (1200px wide). Bottom-left on all headers and hero images (1200px and 600px wide). Width: 140px on 1200px canvases, 100–110px on 600px canvases. Do not resize the logo to fit a composition — position the composition to accommodate the logo at its specified size.

8. **The measurement UI aesthetic over illustration.** Prefer geometric containers, grid structures, data readouts, and form states over figurative or illustrative elements. If an element could appear in a dashboard or observation framework, use it. If it could appear in a "how to be a better leader" stock image, do not.

## 8. What to Avoid

**Golden Honey in any form.** This is not a style guideline — it is an absolute production rule. #eda113 and any visually adjacent warm yellow-amber do not appear in August. Not as a data viz element, not as a border, not as an accent, not as a shadow. If a designer sees it in a source file from Week 1 of July, it does not come forward.

**Gradients as decoration.** A gradient is not the same as the arc motif line. If a gradient is used, it must serve the arc motif — a transition from resolved to unresolved along the motif line. A gradient used to make a background more interesting is wrong. The background is InSync Blue. Period.

**Three or more accent colors in a single asset.** The palette has many colors. The asset does not use all of them. Maximum two accent colors per asset. Ocean Blue and Cool Blue do not appear in the same asset (they are assigned to different asset types for exactly this reason).

**Decorative framing around headlines or numbers.** No rules above and below a headline. No boxes around numbers on the Cisco stat card. No badge shapes around the diagnostic question. The type is the element. Putting a box around it says the type is not strong enough to stand alone. It is.

**Illustration that reads as "learning is fun."** Any icon or element that resembles a lightbulb, a graduation cap, a star, a celebration emoji, or a person in an "aha moment" is wrong. The IQF containers are geometric and functional, not illustrative.

**All-caps headlines.** The only all-caps text in this campaign is the dimension labels inside the IQF containers (LinkedIn Wednesday), where it functions as a category tag. Everywhere else: sentence case. All-caps headlines read as shouting. This campaign does not shout — it measures.

**Any visual that reads as a sales pitch.** No "call now" aesthetic. No urgency markers (countdown timers, flashes, starbursts). The diagnostic register is clinical and confident. It does not persuade — it presents evidence and lets the evidence work.
