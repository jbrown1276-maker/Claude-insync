## 2. Target Audience

**Primary Persona: L&D Leadership**
- Roles: CLO, VP of L&D, Director of Learning, HR Leadership
- Pressure: Proving learning impact to executives, connecting programs to business outcomes
- Emotional State: Frustrated by incomplete data, anxious about budget justification, seeking credibility with leadership
- What They Want: Clear evidence, executive-ready reporting, a defensible story about learning's value

**Secondary Persona: Practitioners**
- Roles: Facilitators, Instructional Designers, Learning Analysts, Training Managers
- Pressure: Delivering sessions that produce visible results, finding practical ways to measure impact
- What They Want: Practical frameworks, measurement tools, and peer-validated approaches they can apply immediately

## 3. Week Strategic Goal

Open August with a stronger, more provocative frame: it's not just that teams aren't measuring the right things — it's that many aren't equipped to execute live learning with the consistency that makes measurement meaningful. Jennifer's article is the sharp edge; Blog 1 is the practical entry point.

The IQF is introduced this week as a measurement model, not an engagement tactic. That distinction is critical to Q3's proof narrative.

---

## 4. Pain Point Framework

**Operational**
- No consistent framework for observing and measuring what's happening inside a live session
- Facilitator quality varies across programs with no standard observation model
- Gap between what learners say in satisfaction surveys and what actually changed in their behavior

**Emotional**
- Uncertainty about whether facilitation quality is as strong as assumed
- The discomfort of not being able to see inside a session to know if learning is really happening
- Wanting to build a stronger measurement practice but not knowing where to start

**Organizational**
- Executive requests for evidence that go beyond survey scores
- No shared language for discussing learning quality across the organization
- Measurement frameworks that exist on paper but don't translate into session-level practice
