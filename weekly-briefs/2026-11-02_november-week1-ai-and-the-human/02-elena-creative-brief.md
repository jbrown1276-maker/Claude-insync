## 1. Campaign Concept — The Big Idea

### Concept name: **Where the line falls**

**The territory.** This reader has watched the list of things only they can do get shorter for two years, one platform release at a time, and nobody has told them where it stops. Every item that was automated was announced to them as a convenience. The cumulative read from inside the role is not convenience. It is a line moving toward them with no marked end.

So we mark it. Not by arguing about whether the line moves, because they have watched it move and they will not believe us. We mark it by saying where it currently falls, specifically enough that they can check it against the last session they ran.

**The strategic reason this is the concept and not a variation on it.** Marcus's Section 3 names the operative move as a split rather than an adjective. A split is a shape. It can be drawn, which means it can arrive before it is read, which is the only way to beat a feed that has trained this reader to scroll past anything that opens with AI. "Where the line falls" is simultaneously the argument, the geometry, and the question we ask back. When the concept and the visual device are the same object, the package cannot drift apart across five assets.

**It also survives November.** Weeks 2 to 4 will most likely move up to a leadership register on the AI-in-L&D cluster. "Where the line falls" asks the same question at any altitude: for the designer, for the facilitator, for the person funding the work. It states a position without locking the register. Nothing in this brief has to be abandoned in Week 2.

**What the creative must make the reader feel or recognize.** One feeling, in this order: *someone has described my actual job accurately, and they did not flinch about the part that is already gone.* Recognition first, and the recognition is purchased by the admission. Only then, second and quieter, professional pride. Not triumph. The register is a colleague saying something true, not an advocate defending them.

**What the creative is NOT doing this week.**

- Not reassuring. Reassurance is our nearest competitor and the gap is narrow. One notch too soft and we are indistinguishable from the content this reader already discounts.
- Not denying. The extractable sentence states the division positively and never negates. We never say what AI will not do to them.
- Not reopening the September proof argument. No measurement gap, no completion rates, no dashboards. November is a new subject, not Q3's fifth month.
- Not launching anything. The InQuire Engagement Framework is existing, the templates are an existing asset we describe accurately, and the certification already ranks position one. Nothing this week is revealed.
- Not selling a credential. The certification never leads a LinkedIn post. It belongs in the newsletter, the email, and the blog's internal link.
- Not teaching the reader their own job. We name what they already know and hand them a vocabulary for it. The voice validates first and systematizes second.

## 2. Creative Pillars

Four pillars. Each one is a requirement with a per-channel manifestation, not a value.

### Pillar 1 — Admission before comfort

Name what is already gone, item by item, before offering anything. Scheduling. Transcription. Chat routing. Issue flagging. The specificity of the list is the proof that the writer has been in the room, and the admission is what buys permission for everything after it. A producer who is told the automation is overstated stops trusting us immediately, because they have watched it happen.

| Channel | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| Carousel | Card 1 is the admission and nothing else. No comfort on card 1. |
| Post 2 | The AI column is stated first and stated flat, with no hedge. |
| Post 3 | Implicit. The authenticity argument only works if the automation is conceded. |
| Newsletter | Opens inside one specific automated moment, in the reader's own calendar. |
| Email | The idea is the division, and the division starts with what AI took. |

### Pillar 2 — The line is visible before it is read

Every asset that carries a graphic renders the division as form, so the argument arrives pre-verbally. Asymmetric split, two columns, one seam. The proportion is the argument: AI's side is narrower and closed, the producer's side is wider and open. At 150 pixels the reader should see an unequal split with one live side before a single word resolves.

| Channel | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| Carousel | The seam device, full construction. Section 3.2. |
| Post 2 | One card, the seam at its most reduced. The division and nothing else. |
| Post 3 | Deliberately absent. Restraint is the argument here. Section 3.5. |
| Newsletter | The anchor image's own construction, not the seam. |
| Email | A single navy rule and one line. No seam. |

### Pillar 3 — The object is a person, never an output

The producer's half of the line always takes a human object. A learner. Someone drifting. The room. Who needs follow-up. Never a transcript, a summary, a flag, a report, a caption, a draft or a log. This is the theme guard raised to a pillar because it is the week's highest-consequence failure and it will not survive as a footnote.

Applies to every asset without exception, in copy and in imagery. Section 5 makes it failable.

### Pillar 4 — Named work, not instinct

The strategic gift of this week is that this reader has a professional skill nobody has given them a vocabulary for. They know who is drifting. They know when the room turns. That knowledge has only ever been called instinct, or being good with people, and none of those survive a budget conversation. InQuire's three dimensions are existing names for exactly that work. Environmental, emotional, intellectual. Named work can be trained, hired for and defended.

### 3.1 Palette reconciliation. Five rulings, and I am making them rather than describing them

**Ruling one. Navy carries forward untouched.** `#032B44` in the image against September's locked `#032B43` is a one-digit JPEG difference. Use `#032B43`. This is the strongest continuity signal available to us and it is free: November looks related to September because the navy is literally the same navy.

**Ruling two. The ground inverts, and this is the biggest change in the system.** September's law was navy on 80% or more of every surface, with navy explicitly forbidden as an accent on light grounds. The anchor image inverts exactly that: near-white dominant, navy as ink. Do not carry September's dominance law forward. November Week 1 is light-dominant, `#F3F4F6` on 80% or more of every surface, navy as the type and rule color. Restate the layout laws for a light system rather than porting them. Two consequences to plan for: white space now has to be built with rules and scale instead of with a dark field, and thumbnail legibility now depends on navy type mass rather than on contrast against navy.

**Ruling three. One blue, and it is the cape's.** The cape is `#0063C3`. September's Cool Blue is `#00B9F5`. Those are roughly fifty hue degrees apart and both would be asking to mean "the signal." Two blues doing one job is not a system, it is a muddle, and it will be most visible exactly where it matters most, on a moment card where the producer's column sits near the hero image. So: **`#0063C3` is November's signal blue and it means the producer's half of the line.** `#00B9F5` is out of service for November Week 1. This governs the rest of the month, so it is a flag for Jeff (Section 9, item 1) and ledger row E-1. My recommendation is firm. The anchor is already approved and the cape is the loudest object in it. The system should agree with the artwork rather than argue with it.

**Ruling four. Golden Honey returns, as furniture, not as argument.** The gold hairline rules in the anchor are Golden Honey at roughly `#E0A420`, and the decisions log is explicit that the Q3 retirement governed an arc that closed on September 26. So gold is back for Q4, and its role in the anchor is precisely defined by how the anchor uses it: two hairline rules at diagonally opposite corners, framing, carrying no meaning. Keep it there. Gold is a 3 to 4 pixel registration rule at the corners of an asset. It is never a fill, never type, never a node, never a CTA, and never marks anything. If gold ever appears to mean something, it has stolen the blue's job.

**Ruling five. Muted gray `#8A97A6` carries forward and takes a new meaning.** In September gray meant what is lost. In November gray means **AI's half of the line: work that is done, closed, and no longer ours to do.** That is not a loss and it must not be rendered as one. No fading, no dissolving, no ghosting toward the edge of the card, nothing that reads as decay. AI's column is flat, calm, complete and slightly quieter than the producer's. A gray that looks mournful turns Pillar 1 into a lament and loses the reader.

**Retired or banned this week.** `#00B9F5` (superseded by `#0063C3` for November). The tee gray `#B0B4C2` and the sticky-note yellow `#FAF193` are photographic incident, not palette. No red or alarm palette. No green and red or traffic-light logic anywhere, ever, and especially not across the seam. No fourth accent.

**The sticky note deserves its own sentence.** It is charming on the hero and it is banned everywhere else, for two reasons. It is a second warm accent competing with gold, and more importantly a sticky note is an annotation object. On a moment card it would read as the producer marking up something that was handed to them, which is the exact governance depiction Section 5 forbids. No sticky notes, no pens, no highlighters, no clipboards, no red marks, in any generated layer or any drawn element.

### 3.2 The carousel. The reconciled device, card count, and geometry

This is the most valuable thing in this brief, so it is specified rather than gestured at.

**Why September's device does not port.** The Week 3 teal thread argued *accumulation*: one node per behavior card across five behavior cards inside a nine-card set, resolving on card 8, so that five signals visibly became one protocol. Its whole meaning was "these separate things are one thing." November's argument is the opposite shape. It is *division*: these things are two things, and here is where they separate. A single vertical spine cannot carry a division. A spine says one thing continues. Port it unchanged and the carousel will argue for unity in the week whose point is the split.

So the thread's geometry survives and its meaning is reassigned. It stops being a spine at the left content edge and becomes **a seam through the card**.

**Card count: seven. I am setting it, and here is the arithmetic.** Three moment cards are fixed by the blog's own structure. The arc needs an admission card to seat Pillar 1, a card where the division actually happens, a card that shows the whole division at once so it becomes portable, and a closing card carrying the question and the CTA. Three plus four is seven. Six would force either the split card into the admission card, which buries the moment the argument turns, or drop the whole-division card, which is the one card a producer screenshots and the one that makes the line quotable. Marcus's range was six to seven and seven is the end of it that keeps both. Card count remains a Jeff confirm item per September's precedent (row E-7); if it changes, the node spacing rescales and the arc holds.

**3.2a The card template. 1080 × 1080, safe zone 72 px, seven fixed zones. Vary content, never chrome.**

1. **Kicker label.** Top left, letterspaced caps at label scale (16 to 20 px), navy at about 88%, with the gold hairline rule directly beneath it. Inherited from the anchor image. On every card, so a screenshotted card still announces where it came from.
2. **Moment name.** The hero. Face 1 Semibold, 44 to 64 px, navy, left-aligned, spanning the **full content width above both columns**. It sits above the seam, not inside either column, because it governs both halves. Largest element on the card.
3. **The seam.** A vertical rule at **38% of the content width from the left**, running the full height of the column zone. Not centered. The asymmetry is the argument and it has to be unmistakable at thumbnail: if a viewer could mistake it for a half-and-half split, move it further left.
4. **AI column.** Left of the seam, narrower. Label "AI handles" at label scale in muted gray. Beneath it two to four short items, Face 1 Medium, muted gray `#8A97A6`, set **one type step smaller** than the producer column's items. Each item carries a short gray tick that meets the seam and **terminates** with a closed end. Flat and calm. No icons.
5. **Producer column.** Right of the seam, wider. Label "You handle" at label scale in navy. Items in navy, Face 1 Medium, one step larger than AI's. One **blue node** (`#0063C3`, a plain filled mark 12 to 16 px) sits on the seam beside this column. At the bottom of the column, the InQuire anchor line in Face 2 serif at small scale.
6. **Progress indicator.** Bottom edge, seven marks, current mark blue and slightly wider, the rest muted gray. September's zone 4 unchanged except the count. Keep it visually distinct from the seam: horizontal, bottom, small.
7. **Brand mark.** Bottom left, at or below kicker-label scale, on every card.

**3.2b The seam device. Geometry and the seven-card arc.**

- **Stroke.** Above the highest node the seam is navy at 2 px. Along the producer's side it is `#0063C3` at 3 px hero, 2 px minimum at thumbnail. AI's ticks are `#8A97A6` at 2 px and never longer than a third of the AI column's width.

The arc:

**The geometry that carries Pillar 3, and it is not optional.** AI's ticks terminate. They are closed marks that stop at the seam. The producer's line continues. **No arrow ever crosses the seam in either direction.** An arrow from AI's column into the producer's column draws a handoff, and a handoff is the governance reading rendered as a diagram. The seam is a boundary, not a pipeline. Nothing flows across it.

**Never, on any card.** A centered split. Equal columns. A checkmark or a cross. Green and red. Icons on the nodes. Gold anywhere except the corner hairline. A second blue object competing with the node. The seam used as a decorative left border. The progress indicator and the seam confused for one another. Any card where the moment name is not the largest element.

**One hand-off detail that will otherwise surface as a bug.** Cards 4 and 5 carry the identical InQuire anchor, "Emotional + Intellectual," because that is the blog's own structure. It will look like a template error. It is not. The differentiation between those two cards lives entirely in the producer-column items, not in the anchor label. Devon, do not vary the anchor to make the cards feel different. Sarah, make the items on card 5 unmistakably post-session work. Row E-9.

### 3.3 Typography

Faces carry forward from September. Three changes.

**Face 1, grotesque sans (Inter or Archivo).** Moment names 44 to 64 px Semibold. Headlines 48 to 56 px. Column items at reading weight, producer's set one step above AI's. Kicker labels 16 to 20 px letterspaced caps. The anchor image's display word is Face 1 at its heaviest weight with tight negative tracking; that treatment belongs to the hero and to the newsletter header, not to card headlines.

**Face 2, humanist serif (Source Serif or Newsreader).** Carries the InQuire anchor line on moment cards, and it is the hero face on Post 3's single card if one is built. Never carries a number, and this week it never carries one because there are no numbers.

Both stand, and they do not conflict, because they govern different things. The rule governs copy: every headline, subhead, hashtag, title tag and meta description in every derived asset is sentence case. The anchor's caps treatment is a pre-approved typographic rendering of a single word on one already-built asset and it licenses nothing downstream. The only caps permitted in derived assets is the letterspaced kicker label at 16 to 20 px, inherited directly from the anchor, and it never grows above label scale. ### 3.6 Layout principles for a light-dominant system

September's ten laws were written for a navy field. These replace them.

1. The ground is light and it stays light. `#F3F4F6` on 80% or more of every surface.
2. Navy is ink, not field. It carries type, rules and the one solid chip inherited from the anchor.
3. The split is asymmetric and the asymmetry is visible at 150 px.
4. One blue object per card. The node, or the CTA chip, never both competing.
5. Gray is complete, not lost. AI's column is calm and closed. Nothing fades or dissolves.
6. Gold frames and means nothing. Two hairlines, diagonal corners, and no other appearance.
7. The moment name governs both columns and sits above the seam.
8. Nothing crosses the seam. No arrows, no lines, no objects, in either direction.
9. Every card stands alone. Kicker, brand mark and seam logic on every card, because a producer will screenshot the moment that matches their own job.
10. Thumbnail first. Validate the full set at 150 px before any refinement pass.

### 3.7 What to avoid, with the reason attached

## 5. Creative Guardrails

### 5.1 The theme guard. Three failable tests, and this is the section to read twice

The month means a person doing work the model cannot do at all. It does not mean a person checking the model's output. Every test below is mechanical and returns a pass or a fail.

**Test 1 — the object test. Applies to every sentence in the producer's half of the argument.**

Take the sentence. Name its object. If the object is a transcript, a summary, a report, a flag, a caption, a draft, a log, a dashboard, an output, a recommendation or anything AI produced, **the sentence has failed.** The object must be a person or a room: a learner, someone drifting, the room's energy, who needs follow-up, who is lost and afraid to say so.

> Fails: "You review what AI flagged and decide what matters." Object is a flag.
> Fails: "AI drafts the follow-up and you make it human." Object is a draft.
> Passes: "You notice the learner who went quiet in minute 8." Object is a learner.
> Passes: "You catch the tone shift when someone is disengaged but trying to hide it." Object is a person.

**Test 2 — the arrow test. Applies to every drawn element and every generated frame.**

Does anything cross the seam, point across it, flow across it, or hand something over it? If yes, it has failed. AI's marks terminate. The producer's line continues. There is no pipeline between them.

**Test 3 — the gaze test. Applies to every generated image.**

Where are the subject's eyes? If they are directed at a screen, a page, a printout or downward at a surface, the frame has failed, because a lowered gaze reads as reading and reading is the governance depiction. Eyes go up and out, at a person.

**The positive statement all three protect.** The producer is not checking AI's transcript. The producer is watching the learner.
