# Correction needed before publish — Blog 1, Sundlo attribution

**Blog:** "The Producer's New Superpower" — Jen Finan
**Publishes:** Monday, 2026-11-02
**Raised:** 2026-09-23, during November Week 1 copy review
**Severity:** Correct before publish. Not a blocker on the campaign package, which no longer depends on it.

---

## What is wrong

The blog credits Johannes Sundlo with originating the term "slop." He did not originate it, and nothing credits him with it.

**Two places in the source file carry the claim.**

Body, in "Why This Matters Now":

> "The L&D industry is facing an authenticity moment. As AI generates content at scale, the counter-current is clear: volume without judgment is what **researcher Johannes Sundlo** calls 'slop.'"

Source list at the foot of the piece:

> "Johannes Sundlo — **credited with the term** 'slop' for volume-without-judgment content"

The source-list line states the claim outright. The body line is defensible read literally, since he does use the word, but in that construction it reads as crediting him with the term, and a reader who knows the term's history will take it that way.

## What is actually true

- "Slop," meaning low-quality AI-generated output, surfaced around 2022 in online forums reacting to the first wave of AI image generators.
- It was popularized by a writer publishing as *deepfates*, who described it as "the term for unwanted AI generated content."
- British programmer **Simon Willison** is the person usually credited with bringing it into mainstream use, via his blog in May 2024. He has said explicitly that it was in circulation well before he took it up.
- **It has no single originator.** Fast Company called it the defining word of 2025.
- Johannes Sundlo appears nowhere in the coinage story.

## Who Sundlo actually is

He is real and he is a reasonable person to cite on this subject, just not for this claim. He writes the HR and AI newsletter *FullStack HR*, works in HR and AI adoption, and has published a post titled "Slop is slop." His position is roughly that what matters is not whether a human or a machine produced something but whether it is slop, and that slop is a thinking failure rather than an AI failure.

**"Researcher" is also the wrong descriptor.** He is a practitioner and consultant. The campaign team hit the same problem independently and stripped his title from the copy for that reason.

## Recommended fix

**Body — drop the attribution and keep the word.** "Slop" is common currency now and needs a credit no more than "spam" does:

> "The L&D industry is facing an authenticity moment. As AI generates content at scale, the counter-current is clear: volume without judgment has a name, and the name is slop. Generic, disconnected, untrustworthy."

**Source list — remove the line, or correct it to what is supportable:**

> "Johannes Sundlo, *FullStack HR* — has written on slop in an HR and L&D context"

If a coinage credit is wanted at all, it belongs to no one person; Simon Willison is the closest defensible attribution for popularizing it.

## Why this matters more than usual here

The blog's own argument is about authenticity and about content produced faster than anyone can care about it. A misattribution inside that argument is the most checkable error in the piece, and the readers most likely to catch it are exactly the practitioners the blog is written for.
