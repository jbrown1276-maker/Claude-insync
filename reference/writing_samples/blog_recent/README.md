## Purpose

These files are **reference samples for Sarah the copywriter** — not brand rules.

They show how InSync currently writes: sentence rhythm, paragraph length, how arguments are structured, how we open posts, how we close them, which phrases recur, and what "confident but not salesy" sounds like in practice.

**The brand voice document remains the authority.** If anything in these samples conflicts with the brand voice guidelines, defer to the brand voice document. These samples reflect how we've been writing — they are descriptive, not prescriptive.

---

## What Sarah Should Look For

- **Opening moves:** How do posts start? (Problems stated flatly, questions posed, declarative truths)
- **Paragraph length:** Typically 2–4 sentences. Short. One idea per paragraph.
- **Headers:** Direct, declarative, often structured as a "why" statement
- **Sentence structure:** Mix of short punchy sentences and longer analytical ones
- **Tone:** Authoritative but not academic — confident practitioners speaking to peers
- **CTAs:** How service language is woven in without feeling like a hard sell
- **Data use:** Stats cited briefly and moved past; they support the argument, they don't become the argument

## Dominant Themes in This Period

The blog ran two back-to-back content clusters this quarter:

1. **Managed Learning Services (Feb):** Explaining the MLS operating model, differentiating true partnership from vendor relationships, and making the case for outsourcing as a quality play rather than a cost play.

2. **Live Learning Formula (Mar–May):** A serialized deep dive into InSync's three-part framework (Design & Development → Delivery & Facilitation → Support & Learning Continuity), with individual posts on each component and how they interact.

Both clusters share a structural pattern: open with a problem leaders recognize, build toward a systemic explanation (not a talent/effort explanation), and close with a practical diagnostic question or action step.
